What to put on a photographer invoice

Common lines on a photographer's invoice include:

  • Shoot fee (hourly, half-day or full-day)
  • Editing and retouching
  • Prints, albums or products
  • Image licensing / usage rights
  • Travel to location
  • Booking deposit already paid

Plus the essentials every Australian invoice needs: your business name and ABN, the client's details, a unique invoice number and dates, the total (with 10% GST if you're registered), and your payment details. See how to write an invoice and the tax invoice requirements.

A tip for photographers

Charge and record a booking deposit to lock in the date — enter it in Amount Paid so the final invoice shows the balance. If you license images, list the usage rights on their own line so the scope is clear.

GST and your ABN

You must register for GST once your turnover hits $75,000 a year — then you charge 10% GST and issue a Tax Invoice. Under that, GST is optional and you issue a plain invoice. Either way, always quote your ABN. More: the $75,000 GST threshold and do I need an ABN to invoice.

Frequently asked questions

What should a photographer's invoice include?

Your name and ABN, the client, an itemised list (shoot, editing, products, licensing), the total with GST if registered, and payment details. Note any deposit already paid.

How do I invoice a booking deposit?

Invoice the deposit when the client books, then subtract it from the final invoice using the Amount Paid field.
