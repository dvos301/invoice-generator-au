What to put on a rideshare driver invoice

Common lines on a rideshare or Uber driver's invoice include:

  • Trips / fares for the period
  • Booking and service fees
  • Waiting time
  • Tolls on-charged
  • Cleaning or cancellation fees
  • Platform incentives (if invoiced)

Plus the essentials every Australian invoice needs: your business name and ABN, the client's details, a unique invoice number and dates, the total (with 10% GST if you're registered), and your payment details. See how to write an invoice and the tax invoice requirements.

A tip for rideshare drivers

Ride-sourcing is special: you must register for GST from your very first fare, regardless of income. So always issue a Tax Invoice with 10% GST and keep your records — the ATO watches this industry closely.

GST and your ABN

You must register for GST once your turnover hits $75,000 a year — then you charge 10% GST and issue a Tax Invoice. Under that, GST is optional and you issue a plain invoice. Either way, always quote your ABN. More: the $75,000 GST threshold and do I need an ABN to invoice.

Frequently asked questions

Do Uber and rideshare drivers have to charge GST?

Yes. Taxi and ride-sourcing drivers must register for GST from their first fare, regardless of turnover — the $75,000 threshold does not apply.

What should a rideshare invoice include?

Your name and ABN, the period or trips, the fares and fees, 10% GST, and the total. Keep copies for your BAS and tax return.
