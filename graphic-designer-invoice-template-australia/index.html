What to put on a graphic designer invoice

Common lines on a graphic designer's invoice include:

  • Design fee (per project or hourly)
  • Revisions beyond the included rounds
  • Stock imagery, fonts or assets on-charged
  • Print or production costs
  • Source files / handover
  • Deposit already paid

Plus the essentials every Australian invoice needs: your business name and ABN, the client's details, a unique invoice number and dates, the total (with 10% GST if you're registered), and your payment details. See how to write an invoice and the tax invoice requirements.

A tip for graphic designers

Specify how many revision rounds are included and bill extra rounds as a separate line — it prevents scope creep from eating your margin. Ask for a 50% deposit on new clients and record it against the final invoice.

GST and your ABN

GST registration depends on your turnover and the work you do. For most businesses, the ATO registration threshold is $75,000 in current or projected annual GST turnover; ride-sourcing has a different rule. If you are GST-registered and the supply is taxable, show GST and use “Tax Invoice”. If you are not registered, use “Invoice” and do not charge GST. See our GST guide and ABN guide.

Frequently asked questions

What should a graphic designer's invoice include?

Your name and ABN, the client, an itemised description (design, revisions, assets), the total with GST if registered, payment terms and details.

Do graphic designers charge GST?

Only if registered for GST (mandatory over $75,000 turnover). Otherwise issue a plain invoice with no GST.
