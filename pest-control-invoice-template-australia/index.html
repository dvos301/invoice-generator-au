What should a pest control invoice include?

A pest control invoice should include the ATO basics (your name, ABN, date, description, price and GST) plus four pest-specific details: the pest targeted, the area treated, the product used and the warranty terms. Those four lines are what a homeowner, landlord or facility manager checks when activity comes back.

  • Treatment type and pest: general pest spray, cockroach gel bait, rodent baiting, spider treatment, termite inspection or termite treatment. One line each.
  • Area treated: internal, external perimeter, roof void, sub-floor, garden beds. "Whole house" causes arguments later.
  • Product used: the product name (and active constituent if you record it), so the client has it on file for pets, tenants or food-safety audits.
  • Warranty period and re-treatment terms: for example "6-month warranty on cockroach treatment; free re-treatment if activity returns within the period, excluding new infestations from neighbouring units".
  • Call-out or inspection fee as a separate line, so it is clear what the treatment itself cost.
  • Your licence number and the technician's name, if someone other than you did the job.

Add the essentials every Australian invoice needs: a unique invoice number, the client's details, the property address, payment terms and your bank details. If you are GST-registered, title it "Tax Invoice"; for sales of $1,000 or more the ATO also requires the buyer's identity or ABN. See the full tax invoice requirements.

Should your pest management licence number go on the invoice?

Yes, as good practice: the licence is the first thing a careful client or property manager checks, and putting the number on the invoice saves the phone call. Licensing is state-based, and the licence covers doing the work, not the invoice itself. The three largest states license pest controllers through different regulators:

StateRegulatorWho needs a licence
NSWEnvironment Protection Authority (pest management technician licence)Anyone applying pesticides, other than fumigants, to control pests for a fee or reward. Service NSW
QLDQueensland Health (pest management licence)Anyone applying pesticides or fumigants on a commercial fee-for-service basis. ABLIS
VICDepartment of Health (pest control licence)Anyone carrying on the business of controlling, destroying or repelling pests, with exemptions such as agriculture and horticulture. Health Victoria

Other states and territories run their own schemes. We have not found a rule that makes the licence number a mandatory invoice field, so check your regulator's record-keeping and treatment-notice rules, which sit outside the invoice.

Is a termite inspection report the same as an invoice?

No. A termite or timber pest inspection report records what you found; the invoice bills the client for producing it. Keep them as two documents and put the report number on the invoice so the client, their conveyancer or their insurer can match them.

Pre-purchase timber pest inspections are generally done to the national standard for timber pest inspections and reports, AS 4349.3. The invoice line can simply read "Pre-purchase timber pest inspection and report (AS 4349.3), report #2291, 14 Smith St". If the buyer and the person paying are different (an agent or conveyancer often pays), invoice whoever engaged you and name the property.

How do you invoice quarterly or annual pest control contracts?

Pick one of two models and stick to it: invoice after each scheduled visit, or invoice an agreed instalment at the start of each period. Per-visit invoicing suits homes; commercial clients such as cafes, warehouses and strata usually want one invoice per quarter that quotes their purchase order number and names the service period.

  • Per visit: "Quarterly general pest service, visit 2 of 4, 12 October 2026". Easy to dispute-proof because each invoice matches a job.
  • Per period: "Pest management service, 1 October to 31 December 2026, per agreement #A-118". Good for cash flow, but say what happens if a visit is missed.
  • Annual termite inspection: a one-line reminder invoice each year, sent before the anniversary rather than after.

Commercial accounts are where pest invoices go quiet: the invoice lands in an accounts inbox, waits for PO approval and drifts past 30 days. Do pest controllers need an ABN and GST on invoices?

If you run a pest control business, quote your ABN on every invoice. When a business client pays a supplier who has not quoted an ABN, the ATO generally requires the client to withhold 47% of the payment, so a missing ABN can cost you nearly half the invoice until tax time.

You must register for GST once your GST turnover reaches $75,000 in the current or projected 12 months (see the ATO registration rules). Below that, registration is optional; if you are not registered, do not add GST. More in our GST threshold guide and ABN guide.

Frequently asked questions

What should be on a pest control invoice?

Your business name and ABN, the client and property address, the service date, a unique invoice number, each treatment as its own line (pest targeted, area treated, product used), the warranty period and re-treatment terms, the total with GST if you are registered, and how to pay. Adding your pest management licence number answers the client's first question before they ask it.

Do I need a licence to invoice for pest control?

You need a licence to do the work, not to send the invoice. In NSW the EPA licenses pest management technicians who apply pesticides for a fee or reward, in Queensland you need a Queensland Health pest management licence to apply pesticides or fumigants on a commercial fee-for-service basis, and in Victoria the Department of Health licenses anyone carrying on a pest control business. Check your own state regulator.

Is a termite inspection report the same as an invoice?

No. The report records what you found and is a separate document; the invoice bills for the inspection. Pre-purchase timber pest inspections are generally reported to the Australian Standard AS 4349.3. Reference the report number on the invoice so the two can be matched.

How do I invoice a quarterly pest control contract?

Either invoice after each visit, or invoice the agreed instalment at the start of each quarter. Per-visit invoicing is simplest for homes; commercial clients often want one invoice per quarter quoting their purchase order number. Whichever you choose, name the service period on every invoice.
