What to put on a mechanic invoice

Common lines on a mechanic's invoice include:

  • Labour — hours × workshop rate
  • Parts and components supplied
  • Diagnostic / inspection fee
  • Consumables (oil, filters, fluids)
  • Call-out (mobile mechanic)
  • Deposit already paid

Plus the essentials every Australian invoice needs: your business name and ABN, the client's details, a unique invoice number and dates, the total (with 10% GST if you're registered), and your payment details. See how to write an invoice and the tax invoice requirements.

A tip for mechanics

Itemise parts and labour separately — customers expect to see the breakdown, and it's required practice for warranty and insurance work. Keep every invoice for five years for your records and the ATO.

GST and your ABN

GST registration depends on your turnover and the work you do. For most businesses, the ATO registration threshold is $75,000 in current or projected annual GST turnover; ride-sourcing has a different rule. If you are GST-registered and the supply is taxable, show GST and use “Tax Invoice”. If you are not registered, use “Invoice” and do not charge GST. See our GST guide and ABN guide.

Frequently asked questions

What should a mechanic's invoice include?

Your business name and ABN, the customer and vehicle, itemised labour and parts, any diagnostic fee, the total with 10% GST if registered, and payment details.

Do mechanics need to charge GST?

Yes once turnover reaches $75,000 a year — then charge 10% GST and issue tax invoices.
