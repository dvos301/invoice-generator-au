What to put on a consultant invoice

Common lines on a consultant's invoice include:

  • Consulting fee — hours or days × rate
  • Fixed project / engagement fee
  • Retainer for the period
  • Travel and accommodation
  • Reimbursable expenses
  • Deposit or milestone already paid

Plus the essentials every Australian invoice needs: your business name and ABN, the client's details, a unique invoice number and dates, the total (with 10% GST if you're registered), and your payment details. See how to write an invoice and the tax invoice requirements.

A tip for consultants

Day rates and retainers are common in consulting — put the rate and the number of units clearly on each line. Larger corporate clients often run Net 30, so state a firm due date and follow up promptly if it slips.

GST and your ABN

You must register for GST once your turnover hits $75,000 a year — then you charge 10% GST and issue a Tax Invoice. Under that, GST is optional and you issue a plain invoice. Either way, always quote your ABN. More: the $75,000 GST threshold and do I need an ABN to invoice.

Frequently asked questions

How should a consultant invoice a client?

Itemise your fee (hourly, daily or fixed), add any expenses, show GST if registered, and include clear payment terms and details. Reference any PO number the client gave you.

Do consultants need to register for GST?

Once your turnover reaches $75,000 a year, yes. Then you charge 10% GST and issue tax invoices.
