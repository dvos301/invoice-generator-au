What to put on a freelancer invoice

Common lines on a freelancer's invoice include:

  • Project fee, or hours × hourly rate
  • Milestone / stage payment
  • Revisions or additional scope
  • Expenses (stock, software, subscriptions) on-charged
  • Deposit already paid
  • Retainer for the month

Plus the essentials every Australian invoice needs: your business name and ABN, the client's details, a unique invoice number and dates, the total (with 10% GST if you're registered), and your payment details. See how to write an invoice and the tax invoice requirements.

A tip for freelancers

Agree your payment terms and any deposit before you start, and put them on the invoice. For ongoing clients, a monthly retainer invoice with Net 7 terms keeps your cash flow steady. See our guide on invoice payment terms.

GST and your ABN

You must register for GST once your turnover hits $75,000 a year — then you charge 10% GST and issue a Tax Invoice. Under that, GST is optional and you issue a plain invoice. Either way, always quote your ABN. More: the $75,000 GST threshold and do I need an ABN to invoice.

Frequently asked questions

Do freelancers charge GST in Australia?

Only if registered for GST, which is mandatory once you turn over $75,000 a year. Under that, it's optional and you issue a plain invoice.

What should a freelancer invoice include?

Your name and ABN, the client, an itemised description of the work or project, the total (plus GST if registered), payment terms and your payment details.

Can I invoice for a deposit?

Yes — invoice the deposit up front, then record it in Amount Paid on the final invoice so it shows the remaining balance.
