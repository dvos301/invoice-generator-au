How we tested: the commission-split test. Three questions per tool. Can it carry a commission split, a referral fee and a marketing recharge on one invoice as separate line items? Can you control the GST treatment line by line, rather than flipping one 10% switch across the document? And can it wait — commission lands at settlement, weeks after a contract goes unconditional — then chase the invoice once the due date passes?

Here is the arithmetic behind the test, as an illustration rather than a standard: rates and splits vary by agency, state and individual agreement. On a $750,000 sale at 2.2%, the gross commission is $16,500. On a 50/50 split with the agency the agent invoices $8,250, which is $8,250 plus $825 GST and $9,075 payable if you are registered. Add a $900 marketing recharge to the vendor as its own line with its own GST treatment, and one settlement has produced a three-line document. Run your own figures through the GST calculator.

Who fails the test. Question one is table stakes: all eight put three lines on one invoice. Question two thins the field out completely — none of the eight publishes line-by-line GST control on the Australian pages we checked at the time of writing, so if a recharge is treated differently from the commission, confirm it on a trial or raise it as a second invoice. Question three separates them. Only InvoiceSonic on Pro and Bookipi list recurring or scheduled invoicing, and only InvoiceSonic, Square and Bookipi list automatic reminders. A free PDF generator fails outright: it builds the document, it never sends it.

What to look for in invoicing software for real estate agents

Agent billing is low-frequency and high-value: a handful of documents a year, each worth thousands, each waiting on a settlement date you do not control. That inverts the usual priorities — payment fees and timing matter far more than how many invoices a plan allows.

  • Unlimited line items, not a single-service template. One settlement can produce a split, a referral fee and a recharge. Three lines on one document beats three documents the agency has to reconcile.
  • Line-level GST control, tested before you commit. No tool here publishes it, so make it the question you ask on the trial, and be ready to raise a differently treated recharge separately.
  • A due date that survives a moving settlement. Settlements slip, so you want to re-date an invoice without rebuilding it. Our guide to invoice payment terms in Australia covers what to put on the document.
  • The payment method and its fee. On a $9,075 invoice, PayID and bank transfer cost nothing and clear overnight; 2.2% on a card costs $199.65 of the same money.
  • A proper Australian tax invoice. Registered for GST, it must carry the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of the supply, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more — see the full tax invoice requirements and the ATO. With no ABN, the payer may be required to withhold 47%.
  • Numbering you can defend a year later. Commission invoices get matched against settlement statements at tax time, so use a sequence you can explain — see how to number invoices.

Which one should you choose?

Price it against ten settlements a year at $8,250 plus GST. At the time of writing that costs A$0 with the free generator or Zoho Invoice, A$168 a year with InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually, $99 a year with Rounded Solo, US$9.99 a month with Bookipi Starter, and $1,996.50 a year in card fees with Square if every settlement is paid by card. The cheapest subscription is not the cheapest outcome — the payment rail decides that. Before you pick anything, ask the agency whether they raise the tax invoice at their end, because some do, and you do not want two documents chasing one commission.

  • If you settle a few properties a year and bill nothing else → the free Australian tax invoice generator, and email the PDF.
  • If you invoice commission, referrals and recharges regularly and want them chased → InvoiceSonic on Pro.
  • If you want sending and a saved agency list for nothing → Zoho Invoice, and accept the dense interface.
  • If irregular five-figure income is your real problem → Rounded, for the tax estimate as much as the invoicing.
  • If a private client will only pay by card → Square Invoices, with the 2.2% written into your fee.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. The clearest case is trust money. If you are an agency holding deposits, rent or sale proceeds on behalf of somebody else, that money is governed by strict state-based rules covering receipting, banking, reconciliation, audit and record retention, and it must never be run through a generic invoicing app — no tool on this list, ours included, is built or licensed for it. Trust accounting and property-management platforms such as PropertyMe, Console, Rex and Agentbox are a separate, regulated category, and that is the shortlist for rent rolls, disbursements and trust reconciliation. Your own commission invoice to the agency is a different thing, and an ordinary invoicing tool handles it fine. Beyond trust money: if you want tax and expenses managed alongside the invoicing, Rounded is Australian-built for sole traders and Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing, cheaper than InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually. If the office bookkeeper already runs Xero or MYOB and lodges the BAS, that is accounting software territory. And a free PDF generator stops being the right answer the moment the same invoice has to be sent, tracked and chased.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best invoicing software for real estate agents in Australia?

InvoiceSonic. A commission invoice is a multi-line document that has to wait for settlement and then chase itself, and InvoiceSonic Pro is the only tool here that lists recurring or scheduled invoicing, automatic reminders and open tracking together. Bookipi lists recurring invoicing and overdue reminders too, including on its free plan, but that plan stops at 3 invoices a month.

How do you invoice a commission split as a real estate agent?

As separate line items on one tax invoice: your share of the gross commission, any referral fee, and any marketing recharge, each described plainly with the property address and the settlement date. As an illustration, a $750,000 sale at 2.2% is $16,500 gross, so a 50/50 split has the agent invoicing $8,250 plus GST. Ask the agency first whether they raise the tax invoice at their end, so you do not issue a second document for the same commission.

Do real estate agents have to charge GST on commission invoices?

Once you are registered for GST, which is compulsory at A$75,000 of turnover, your invoices must be tax invoices and carry 10% GST unless your accountant tells you a line is treated differently. At $8,250 a settlement, nine settlements is $74,250 and just under the threshold, and the tenth takes you to $82,500 and over it. Confirm your own treatment with the ATO or your accountant.

Can I use invoicing software for a real estate trust account?

No. Trust money is governed by strict state-based rules covering receipting, banking, reconciliation, audit and record retention, and it must not be handled through a generic invoicing app. Trust accounting and property-management platforms such as PropertyMe, Console, Rex and Agentbox are a separate, regulated category. Your own commission invoice to the agency is a different thing and can be raised in an ordinary invoicing tool.

What has to be on a real estate agent's tax invoice in Australia?

If you are registered for GST the ATO requires the words Tax invoice, your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. A commission invoice clears that last one every time, so the agency's ABN belongs on the document. Add the property address and the settlement date so the agency can match it.
