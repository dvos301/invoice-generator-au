How to Invoice as an Electrician: Line Items, Markup, Certificates and GST

IG
The Invoice-Generator.com.au team 16 October 2026 · 10 min read

How to invoice as an electrician: split the job into a call-out fee, labour and materials, mark up the materials on purpose, add your ABN and licence number, reference the compliance certificate, and add 10% GST if you're registered. The fastest way to get the layout right is a free electrician invoice template; this guide covers the decisions behind each line, with a worked switchboard invoice and the markup maths most sparkies get wrong.

For the general list of what every valid tax invoice must show, see tax invoice requirements in Australia. For the yes/no rule on charging GST, see do I add GST to my invoice? This page is about what's specific to electrical work.

What should an electrician's invoice itemise?

An electrician's invoice should show the call-out or service fee, labour, and materials as separate lines, plus the job address and the certificate number for the work. Customers argue about lumped totals; they rarely argue about a $95 call-out next to 6.5 hours of labour and a parts list.

LineHow to write itWhy it's separate
Call-out / service fee"Service call, incl. first 30 min" or "Call-out fee"Covers travel and diagnosis; it's what you charge even when the fix is quick
Labour (hourly)"Labour: 6.5 h × $105.00"Shows the customer what they paid for; easy to check against the quote
Labour (fixed job)"Supply and install 2 × downlights, fixed price"For quoted, repeatable jobs where hours don't matter to the customer
MaterialsItem, quantity, unit price: "8 × RCBO 20A @ $60.00"Lets you apply markup per item and answers "what did I pay for?"
After-hours loading"After-hours rate, Sat 7pm" as its own lineA visible surcharge is easier to defend than a higher hourly rate
Testing / certificate"Test and certify, certificate no. ____"Links the invoice to the safety paperwork the customer must receive

Hourly or fixed? Use fixed pricing for jobs you can scope from a photo (power points, downlights, smoke alarm swaps) and hourly for fault-finding, where nobody knows how long it'll take. Whatever you quoted is how you invoice: if the quote said "fixed price", don't send an hourly breakdown that adds up to more.

How do I work out materials markup on an electrical invoice?

Markup is a percentage added to your cost; margin is the share of the selling price that's profit. A 25% markup is a 20% margin, not 25%. Decide which number you're targeting before you price the parts list, because the gap grows with every job.

Materials costMethodSell priceProfitMargin
$640.0025% markup: $640 × 1.25$800.00$160.0020.0%
$640.0025% margin: $640 ÷ 0.75$853.33$213.3325.0%
$640.00No markup ("at cost")$640.00$0.000%

The last row is the common one: materials passed through at trade price. You've spent time sourcing, collecting and warrantying those parts, and you carry the cost until the customer pays. How much you add is your call; there's no regulated rate. What matters is that the invoice shows a unit price per item, so a customer comparing a $60 RCBO with a hardware-store shelf price sees a clear line, not a mystery total.

Markup goes on the GST-exclusive cost. If you're registered, you claim the GST on your wholesaler invoice as a credit, then charge GST on the full sell price. If you're not registered, your cost is the GST-inclusive price you paid, and that's what you mark up.

Does an electrician need a licence number on invoices and quotes?

The state rules we checked put the licence-number duty on advertising and certificates, not specifically on invoices. Printing your licence or contractor number on every quote and invoice is still the simplest way to stay covered, and customers can use it to check you on the regulator's register.

StateLicence number rule we verifiedRegulator
NSWThe licensee's name and licence number must be shown in all advertising, and electrical wiring work of any value needs a contractor licence to advertise itNSW building trade advertisements

QLDRegistered name and electrical contractor licence number wherever you advertise; the licence number must also appear on the certificate you give the customerWorkSafe Queensland (Electrical Safety Office)

Note the regulator for electricians in Victoria is Energy Safe Victoria, not the building and plumbing regulator. In NSW, a bigger job can bring contract rules into play too: Fair Trading's guide to home building contracts requires a written contract showing your licence number for residential building work over $5,000. Check whether your job is covered before you start.

Which compliance certificate goes with an electrical invoice?

Every state requires a safety certificate for regulated electrical work, and it's a separate document from the invoice. Reference its number on the invoice so the customer, their insurer or a future buyer can match the payment to the certified work.

  • NSW: Certificate of Compliance for Electrical Work (CCEW). The person who did the test must submit it as soon as practical and no later than seven days after the test, and copies can go automatically to the customer when their email is on the certificate. Lodgement is with Building Commission NSW, per the NSW electrical compliance requirements.
  • VIC: Certificate of Electrical Safety (COES). Energy Safe Victoria says the customer gets a copy within 30 days for non-prescribed work (lights, power points, air conditioning), and within 4 days after the inspection for prescribed work (consumer mains, main switchboard, solar), which can't be switched on until a licensed inspector has checked it. See ESV's COES page for consumers.
  • QLD: certificate of testing and compliance (installation work) or certificate of testing and safety (work on equipment), given to the customer as soon as possible after the work. Contractors keep a copy for five years.

Practical rule: don't send the invoice before the certificate exists. If the certificate number is on the invoice, the customer has no reason to hold payment while they "wait for the paperwork".

Should electricians use progress invoices on big jobs?

Yes, on anything that runs for weeks or ties up thousands in materials, such as new-build fit-outs, rewires and commercial jobs. Agree the stages in the quote, then invoice each stage when it's done, so you're never funding the builder's project from your own account.

A common split for a house fit-out quoted at $18,400 + GST:

StageShareEx GSTGSTInvoice total
Rough-in complete40%$7,360.00$736.00$8,096.00

Final test and certificate10%$1,840.00$184.00$2,024.00
Total100%$18,400.00$1,840.00$20,240.00

Each progress invoice should name the stage, show what's been billed so far and what's left, and carry its own number in sequence (see how to number invoices). Common mistakes on electrical invoices

  • Lumping parts into labour. "Switchboard upgrade: $1,577.50" invites "why so much?". Itemised lines don't.
  • Confusing markup with margin. Pricing for a 25% margin with a 25% markup leaves $53.33 behind on every $640 of materials.
  • No certificate reference. The customer chases the certificate instead of paying the invoice.
  • Invoicing a fixed-price job by the hour. If the quote said $480 fixed, the invoice says $480, plus any written variations.
  • Missing the customer on a large tax invoice. At $1,000 or more, add the buyer's name or ABN.
  • No due date. "Due on completion" or "7 days" belongs on the invoice; see invoice payment terms in Australia.

The short version

Itemise call-out, labour and materials; mark up materials on purpose; put your ABN and licence number on every document; reference the certificate; add GST if you're registered; and split long jobs into progress invoices agreed in the quote. Frequently asked questions

What should an electrician's invoice include?

Your business name, ABN and licence number, the customer and job address, the date and a unique invoice number, then separate lines for the call-out fee, labour (hours × rate or a fixed job price) and materials. If you're registered for GST, head it 'Tax Invoice' and show the GST. Reference the compliance certificate number for the job.

Do electricians have to put their licence number on invoices?

The state rules we checked target advertising and certificates rather than invoices. NSW requires the licensee's name and licence number in all advertising, Victoria requires the REC number on promotional material including advertisements, notices and statements, and Queensland requires the contractor licence number in advertising and on the certificate. Printing it on every quote and invoice is simple good practice.

How much markup should an electrician put on materials?

There's no set rate; it's a business decision. Just know the maths: a 25% markup on $640 of materials gives $800, which is only a 20% margin. To make a 25% margin you divide cost by 0.75, which gives $853.33.

Is the compliance certificate the same as the invoice?

No. The certificate (a CCEW in NSW, a Certificate of Electrical Safety in Victoria, a certificate of testing and compliance or testing and safety in Queensland) is a separate safety document with its own rules. Put its number on the invoice so the customer can match the two.

When should an electrician use progress invoices?

On longer jobs such as new-build fit-outs or rewires, where you'd otherwise carry weeks of labour and materials. Agree the stages in the quote (for example rough-in, fit-off, final) and invoice each stage as it's completed.
