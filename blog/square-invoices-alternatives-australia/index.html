Read the "lands per $1,000" column carefully. It assumes the client pays by bank transfer or PayID, because that is the only way $1,000 invoiced becomes $1,000 received. Every figure here is at the time of writing, and where a tool does not publish an Australian card rate we have not invented one — confirm it on a trial before you move your billing.

How we tested: the take-home test. Three questions per option. For every $1,000 invoiced, how much lands in your account? What does the tool itself cost across a normal trading year? And does it produce a proper Australian tax invoice — ABN, 10% GST where you are registered — without a workaround?

Square Invoices is free to send with, so the subscription is not the cost. At 2.2% on online card payments at the time of writing, $1,000 invoiced returns $978. Scale that to a full year. A sole trader billing $120,000 entirely through card links pays $2,640 in fees and banks $117,360. The same $120,000 invoiced for bank transfer or PayID returns the full amount, and the only cost is the software: A$168 for a year of InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually at the time of writing, leaving $119,832. That is A$2,472 a year of difference on identical work.

The break-even is lower than most people guess. A$168 is 2.2% of A$7,636, so once more than roughly $7,640 of your annual billing goes through a card link, the card fee has already cost more than a whole year of a paid invoicing subscription. In invoices rather than percentages: eight $1,000 jobs paid by card cost $176 in fees, more than that year of InvoiceSonic Pro. One $5,000 job costs $110 on its own. Add Square's Plus plan at AUD $30 a month and the card-heavy year is $360 plus $2,640, or $3,000.

No software makes a card network free. That is the part most comparison posts skate over. There are two separate fees in play: the tool's own cut, and the payment rail's cut. Square's 2.2% is the rail. Any alternative that offers a card button is paying a rail too, whether or not it publishes the rate. The thing you can actually change is which rail your invoice asks for. A bank transfer or PayID moves bank to bank, never passes through the software, and so has nothing deducted from it — which is why every "lands per $1,000" figure above is a bank-transfer figure.

Who fails the take-home test. Only one option here loses more of a $1,000 invoice than Square does: PayPal Invoicing, whose per-transaction cut is the highest of anything on this page. Two more fail on the second question rather than the first. A free PDF generator takes nothing and chases nobody — it builds the document and stops. Xero returns the full $1,000 but has no free plan, so the year is not cheap for one person sending a dozen invoices a month. Rounded also has no free tier. Everything else takes zero from a bank transfer.

Who should stay on Square. Square is not a bad product and 2.2% is not a defect, it is a price. You get unlimited free invoices, estimates and automatic reminders without a subscription, and money from a card link often lands the same or the next day rather than whenever your client next opens their banking app. If cash flow is tight, being paid on Tuesday instead of on the 30th is worth $22 per $1,000. Stay on Square if your clients are consumers who tap a card, if you already take payments on Square hardware in person, or if chasing bank transfers costs you more in unpaid hours than the fee costs in dollars. Leave when most of your clients are businesses that would happily pay to a BSB and account number anyway.

1. InvoiceSonic — Best overall

InvoiceSonic is the closest like-for-like replacement for Square Invoices: it builds a professional tax invoice in about a minute, emails it, tracks whether it was opened, and chases it automatically if nobody pays. The difference is where the money goes — bank transfer and PayID are supported with no gateway to configure and no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so a $1,000 invoice arrives as $1,000.

On the take-home test it is the strongest result on this page: full amount in, and a known, fixed annual cost. Pro adds recurring invoices, custom branding with your own logo and colours, invoice open tracking, online quote acceptance that converts a quote into an invoice in one click, and email receipts the moment you mark something paid. Australian formatting is built in — ABN field, BSB, the 10% GST label — as part of localised support across 19 countries. You can also take a deposit as a percentage or a fixed amount up front, which does more for cash flow than a fast card settlement does. Subscription billing runs through Stripe and full card details are never stored.

Pros

  • No InvoiceSonic transaction fee on bank transfer or PayID, so the take-home on $1,000 is $1,000.
  • Annual Pro is price-locked: the rate never rises while the subscription is active.
  • Deposits and progress claims up front, plus automatic reminders on the invoices you have already sent.

Cons

  • The free plan is capped at 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month, so anyone leaving Square's unlimited free invoices will need Pro.
  • No native Android app — Android users add the web app to the home screen — and the 7-day Pro trial requires a card up front.

Price: at the time of writing, the free plan is A$0 and the first invoice needs no sign-up and no credit card; Pro is A$19 a month billed monthly, or A$168 a year billed annually, which works out at A$14 a month and saves A$60. Best for: sole traders and small businesses whose clients will pay by transfer, who want the invoice sent and chased without losing 2.2% of it.

3. Zoho Invoice — Best free tool that still sends

Zoho Invoice is free — Zoho states no cost and no contracts — and unlike a PDF generator it sends the invoice, stores the client and keeps the history. It is the most direct free-for-free swap from Square Invoices on this page.

It passes the take-home test on a bank transfer and costs nothing across a full year, which is a hard combination to argue with. The trade-off is the surroundings. Zoho Invoice is the front door to a very large product suite, the interface is dense next to a single-purpose app, and support is really aimed at customers paying for the wider suite. Multi-language invoicing across 19-plus languages is genuinely useful if you bill clients overseas. Check how it handles Australian GST and your ABN on your first invoice rather than assuming it, and pair it with sensible invoice payment terms so the free reminders have a due date to work from.

Pros

  • Free at the time of writing, with sending, a saved client list and invoice history included.
  • Multi-language invoicing across 19-plus languages for clients outside Australia.

Cons

  • The interface is dense, and the free product sits inside a large upsell suite.
  • Real support is a paid-suite affair, so a billing problem on the free tier is your problem.

Price: free at the time of writing. Best for: people who want Square's sending and tracking for A$0 and can live with a busy interface.

4. Bookipi — Best on a tiny budget with recurring invoices

Bookipi is a simple invoice app that syncs between web and mobile, and it lists overdue reminders and recurring invoices even on its free plan, which is unusual at that price. If what you actually valued in Square was the automatic reminder, this is the cheapest place to get it back.

The take-home on a bank transfer is the full $1,000. The catch is volume and currency. Three free invoices a month is very few — if you send ten, you are on Starter within a fortnight — and Starter is priced in US dollars, so the line on your bank statement moves with the exchange rate. Run the year: US$9.99 a month is US$119.88 across twelve months, and what that costs you in AUD is not knowable in advance. Against Square's 2.2%, Bookipi Starter still wins on anything above about eight card-paid $1,000 invoices a year, but you are trading a percentage for a currency risk.

Pros

  • Recurring invoices and overdue reminders even on the free plan.
  • Web and mobile stay in sync, so an invoice started on the phone finishes on the laptop.

Cons

  • Three free invoices a month is far too few for a working business.
  • Priced in USD, so your monthly cost changes with the AUD even though nothing else did.

Price: at the time of writing, free for 3 invoices a month; Starter US$9.99 per month; Professional US$52 per month. Best for: part-timers and side businesses who want reminders back without paying a percentage.

5. Wave — Best free invoicing with basic books attached

Wave is free in Australia at the time of writing and bundles invoicing with light accounting, so you get a ledger alongside the invoice list. On the take-home test it returns the full $1,000 on a bank transfer at no software cost, which on paper is the best possible result.

The honest caveat is that Wave is built around North America. Its payments and payroll features are US and Canada-centric, so the practical Australian use is invoicing for a bank transfer rather than collecting money inside the app — which is fine for this test, and a poor swap if the card link was the whole point of Square for you. GST handling is generic rather than shaped around what the ATO expects, so check your first invoice against the tax invoice requirements line by line. Australian support is thin.

Pros

  • Free in Australia at the time of writing, with invoicing and basic bookkeeping in one place.
  • No subscription and no tool-side cut, so a full year of invoicing costs nothing.

Cons

  • GST is generic rather than ATO-shaped, so compliance is on you to verify.
  • Payments and payroll are built for the US and Canada, and Australian support is thin.

Price: free in Australia at the time of writing. Best for: cost-conscious sole traders who want a ledger as well as invoices and will be paid by transfer.

6. Rounded — Best Australian-built option for sole traders

Rounded is built in Australia specifically for sole traders and freelancers, and it covers invoicing, expenses and tax estimates in one place. For someone who has never set money aside for tax, that estimate is often worth more than the fee they saved by leaving Square.

It returns the full $1,000 on a transfer and the annual cost is knowable: Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing, which is cheaper than InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually and cheaper than 2.2% on $4,500 of card billing. That is a real reason to pick it over our number one, and we would rather say so than pretend otherwise. There is no free plan, so you cannot run a quiet month on it to see whether it fits, and promotional pricing varies, so check what year two costs before you commit.

Pros

  • Australian-built for sole traders, so expenses and tax estimates are shaped for the ATO rather than translated.
  • Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing — cheaper than most paid options here.

Cons

  • No free plan at all, so there is no zero-cost way to trial your real billing on it.
  • Promotional pricing varies, and Pro at $70 a month regular is a big step up from Solo.

Price: at the time of writing, Solo $99 per year regular and Pro $70 per month regular in AUD; promotional pricing varies. Best for: Australian sole traders who want invoicing, expenses and a tax estimate from one local app.

7. Xero — Best if your bookkeeper is already in it

Xero is accounting software with invoicing inside it, not an invoicing app, and that distinction decides whether it belongs on your shortlist. It returns the full $1,000 on a bank transfer, and it is the right answer when your bookkeeper already works in Xero and wants invoices, bank feeds, GST and BAS in one system instead of three.

For one person leaving Square because of a percentage, it is a heavy swap. There is no free plan and no way to test a month for nothing, and you are buying a general ledger to solve a payments problem. The picture flips once you have staff, a bookkeeper and a quarterly BAS, because you stop paying somebody to re-key invoices from one system into another. Pros

  • Invoicing, bank reconciliation, GST and BAS in one system your accountant already knows.

  • No free plan, and considerably more software than a solo operator needs.
  • It solves bookkeeping, not payment fees — the card rate you pay still depends on whatever gateway you connect.

Price: paid plans available — check current pricing. Best for: businesses whose books are already run in Xero by a bookkeeper.

8. PayPal Invoicing — Best only if the client insists on PayPal

PayPal Invoicing is free to send with, has no monthly fee, and is the one option here that does worse than Square on the take-home test. PayPal's per-transaction cut is the highest of anything on this page, so $1,000 invoiced lands below Square's $978.

It is on the list because sometimes it is the only thing that works. Overseas clients who already hold a PayPal balance will pay a PayPal invoice today and argue about an international bank transfer for a week, and for a small overseas job that speed can outweigh the cut. For Australian clients it is the wrong swap: you would be leaving Square over a percentage and choosing a bigger one, with more friction on the buyer's side because they need a PayPal account or the patience to check out as a guest. Check PayPal's current Australian rate on their own fees page before you assume anything about a specific invoice.

Pros

  • No monthly fee and no subscription — you only pay when you actually get paid.
  • Overseas clients who already use PayPal can pay immediately, without an international transfer.

Cons

  • The highest per-transaction cut of any option on this page, so the take-home is worse than staying on Square.
  • Buyer-side friction, and Australian GST presentation is generic rather than ATO-shaped.

Price: free to send; fees on payment — check current pricing. Best for: occasional overseas invoices to clients who will not pay any other way.

What to look for in a Square Invoices alternative

Leaving Square is a payments decision dressed up as a software decision. The subscription line is usually the smallest number involved, and the rail your invoice asks for is the biggest. Judge candidates in that order.

  • Separate the tool's fee from the rail's fee. A tool that charges $0 a month but routes you into a card gateway can cost more than a A$168-a-year subscription that asks for a bank transfer. Compare take-home, not price pages.
  • Work out your card-paid share first. If only $3,000 of your yearly billing goes through card links, 2.2% is $66 and switching gains you almost nothing. Above roughly $7,640 it exceeds a full year of a paid subscription, and the sums start to matter.
  • Check that reminders survive the move. Automatic reminders are the Square feature people miss most. InvoiceSonic includes them on Pro and Bookipi lists them even on free; a PDF generator has none, and unpaid invoices become your evening job.
  • Confirm PayID and BSB fields, not just "bank transfer". A PayID on the invoice removes the BSB-and-account-number retyping that causes half of all misdirected payments, and it clears fast enough to blunt the cash-flow argument for cards.
  • Make sure it still produces an Australian tax invoice. Registered for GST means the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN on sales of $1,000 or more. Several free tools here are generic on that point.
  • Price the speed you are giving up. Card money often lands same or next day. A bank transfer lands when the client gets around to it, so tighten your terms — 7 or 14 days rather than 30 — before you decide the fee was pure waste.

Which one should you choose?

Run a year at $120,000 of billing. At the time of writing that costs A$0 with the free generator, Zoho Invoice or Wave, A$168 with InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually, about US$119.88 with Bookipi Starter, $99 with Rounded Solo, and $2,640 in card fees on Square if every client taps a card — $3,000 with Plus. The cheapest software is rarely the cheapest year.

  • If you send a few invoices a month and clients pay to a bank account → the free Australian tax invoice generator, and email the PDF.
  • If you want Square's sending, tracking and reminders without the 2.2% → InvoiceSonic on Pro, invoiced for PayID or transfer.
  • If the budget is genuinely zero but you still need to send → Zoho Invoice, and accept the dense interface.
  • If you want an Australian app that also estimates your tax → Rounded, on Solo.
  • If your clients only ever pay by card and cash flow is tight → stay on Square, and add the 2.2% to your prices instead of absorbing it.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. If your customers are walk-up consumers who tap a card, an invoicing app is solving the wrong problem — Square already collects the money at the point of sale, and InvoiceSonic does not do in-person card payments at all. If your budget is truly zero and you still need sending and reminders, Zoho Invoice and Wave are free at the time of writing while a free InvoiceSonic account stops at 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked send and 1 reminder a month. If you want tax and expenses handled alongside invoicing, Rounded is Australian-built for sole traders and Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing, cheaper than InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually. And once you have staff, a bookkeeper and a quarterly BAS, this is accounting territory: Xero or MYOB, not an invoicing app. A free PDF generator, ours included, is the wrong choice the moment you need the invoice to chase itself — it has no bank feeds, no BAS and no reminders, and neither does InvoiceSonic.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best Square Invoices alternative in Australia?

InvoiceSonic. It sends and chases invoices the way Square does, and because it supports bank transfer and PayID with no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, $1,000 invoiced arrives as $1,000. At the time of writing Pro is A$168 a year billed annually, which is less than 2.2% of A$7,640 of card-paid billing.

How much does Square Invoices really cost per $1,000 invoiced?

At the time of writing Square charges 2.2% on online card payments, so $1,000 invoiced returns $978 and a $5,000 job returns $4,890. Sending the invoice is free and the Plus plan is AUD $30 a month, but on card-heavy billing the percentage is the number that matters, not the plan.

Is there an invoicing app with no transaction fees at all?

No software can make a card network free. What you can avoid is the tool's own cut: InvoiceSonic charges no transaction fee on bank transfer or PayID, because that money moves bank to bank and never passes through the software. If the client insists on a card, someone is paying the rail.

Should I stay on Square Invoices?

Stay if cash flow is tight and your clients pay by card. Unlimited free invoices, automatic reminders and card money that often lands the same or next day are worth 2.2% when the alternative is waiting 30 days. Leave when most clients would happily pay by bank transfer anyway.

Does a card payment change what has to be on the tax invoice?

No. If you are registered for GST the ATO still requires the words Tax invoice, your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. Invoice the full amount and treat the 2.2% as a business expense, not a discount.
