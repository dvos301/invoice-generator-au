How we tested: the receipt-for-rebate test. Three questions per tool. Can it produce one document carrying everything a private client's claim depends on — provider details, the Medicare item number for the service you provided, the session date and the amount paid? Does it store that document, so a client who rings on Wednesday gets it re-sent, not rebuilt? And can you set the tax treatment invoice by invoice, so a GST-free service does not carry a 10% line?

The worked number. In private practice the document is part of the product: the client pays A$230 in full at the end of the session and then uses your paperwork to claim, so a wrong field stalls the claim and you hear about it mid-week. A caseload of 22 private sessions a week across 44 working weeks is 968 documents a year. If one in twenty comes back — a wrong service date, a missing provider number — that is 48 reissues a year. Rebuilding each at six minutes is 288 minutes, or 4.8 hours; re-sending a stored copy takes thirty seconds, so the same 48 cost 24 minutes. Stored history is worth roughly 4.4 hours a year.

Who fails the test. A free PDF generator, ours included, passes the first question and fails the second outright: it stores nothing, so every reissue is a retype. InvoiceSonic's free plan and Bookipi's free plan cap saved invoices low enough that a real caseload burns through them in week one. Wave is weakest on the third question: its GST handling is generic rather than ATO-shaped. Zoho Invoice, Square Invoices, Xero and Rounded all send, store and re-send properly, and none of the eight is a claiming system.

What to look for in invoicing software for psychologists

Psychology billing is low volume and high consequence: twenty-two documents a week is nothing, but each one is a person's claim, and a mistake costs you a client email rather than a late payment.

  • Stored history you can re-send. This is the whole test: it turns 48 reissues a year from 4.8 hours into 24 minutes, and March's copy is identical to July's.
  • Room for the details a claim depends on. Provider details, the Medicare item number for the service you provided, the session date and the amount paid must sit on one document without a workaround. Current requirements come from Services Australia and your professional body, not a template you downloaded.
  • Control of the tax line, invoice by invoice. Most psychology services are GST-free as a health service, so the invoice usually carries no GST line even when the practice is registered. Whether that applies to a given service is a question for the ATO or your accountant; our GST calculator helps only once you know the treatment.
  • A receipt, not just an invoice. When a client pays at the end of the session, the useful document shows the amount paid and the date paid, not an amount owing. Some tools email that automatically; others leave it to you.
  • An ATO-shaped tax invoice underneath. Where you are registered for GST the ATO expects the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. Full list in our tax invoice requirements guide.
  • Where the client's data lives. An invoice here names a person and a service, so ask where the file sits and what happens if you stop paying — with InvoiceSonic, historical invoices stay accessible after a downgrade.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. If what you need is an appointment book, clinical notes and claiming in one place, none of these eight is the answer: practice management systems such as Halaxy, Cliniko and Power Diary are a different category built for exactly that. With a second clinician, payroll and a quarterly BAS you are in accounting territory, and Xero or MYOB is the honest recommendation. If a tax estimate matters more than stored receipts, Rounded is Australian-built for sole traders and costs less per year. A free PDF generator, ours included, is wrong the moment a client asks for the same document twice. And no app makes your billing correct on its own: confirm what a claim requires with Services Australia and your professional body, and your GST position with the ATO or your accountant. Frequently asked questions

What is the best invoicing software for psychologists in Australia?

InvoiceSonic. It keeps a secure cloud history of every invoice, so a client who has lost their paperwork gets the same document re-sent rather than rebuilt, and it emails a receipt when you mark a session paid. Zoho Invoice does the sending and storing for free at the time of writing.

What has to be on a psychologist's invoice for a client to claim a rebate?

Broadly, the claim depends on the document showing the provider's details, the Medicare item number for the service you provided, the session date, the amount charged and paid, and your practice identity and ABN. The requirements change, so confirm the current list with Services Australia and your professional body.

Do psychologists charge GST on their invoices?

Most psychology services are GST-free as a health service, so the invoice usually shows no GST line even when the practice is registered. Whether that applies to your services is not something an app can decide, so confirm your position with the ATO or your accountant before setting a default tax rate.

Is there a free invoicing tool for psychologists?

Yes. Invoice-Generator.com.au is free with no signup, no watermark and no invoice limit, and Zoho Invoice and Wave are both free in Australia at the time of writing. A free InvoiceSonic account gives you 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked send and 1 reminder a month.

Do I need practice management software instead of an invoicing app?

If you want online bookings, clinical notes and claiming in the same system, yes: Halaxy, Cliniko and Power Diary are built for that and an invoicing app is not. If your diary lives in a calendar and your notes in a clinical record you trust, an invoicing app is cheaper and faster for the billing.
