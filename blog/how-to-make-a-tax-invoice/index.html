How to Make a Tax Invoice: A Step-by-Step Walkthrough for GST-Registered Businesses

IG
The Invoice-Generator.com.au team 16 October 2026 · 8 min read

How to make a tax invoice, in short: set up your business details and numbering once, then for each sale put "Tax Invoice" at the top, add the buyer, list what you sold, show the GST, total it, add how to pay and send it as a PDF. This guide walks through that build for a GST-registered business, with a worked $3,256 example. This is the build-it-now walkthrough. For the ATO's list of required details and nothing else, see tax invoice requirements in Australia.

Tax invoice or invoice: which do I make?

Make a tax invoice if you're registered for GST and the sale includes taxable items. If you're not registered, make a plain invoice headed "Invoice", with no GST and without the words "tax invoice". The ATO says unregistered businesses should not include the words "tax invoice".

The 28-day and $82.50 rule comes from the ATO's tax invoices page. In practice, most GST-registered businesses send a tax invoice with every sale so the question never comes up. Not sure whether GST applies to you at all? Start with do I add GST to my invoice?

What do I set up once before my first tax invoice?

Five things: your business name, ABN, contact details, payment details and an invoice numbering scheme. Get them right once and every tax invoice after that is just the buyer, the items and the date.

  1. Business name and ABN. Use the legal or registered business name that matches your ABN record. The ABN is 11 digits; most people write it in the 12 345 678 901 pattern.
  2. Contact details. Address, email and phone, so the buyer knows who to ask about the invoice.
  3. Payment details. BSB and account number, PayID, or both. Our guide to bank details on an invoice covers what to show and how to avoid payment-redirection scams.
  4. Numbering. Pick a format such as INV-0001 and never reuse a number. See how to number invoices for formats that stay tidy.
  5. Default terms. Decide your standard due date (7, 14 or 30 days) and write it the same way every time. Shorter terms get you paid faster; just be consistent.

How do I make a tax invoice, step by step?

Work from the top of the page down: heading and your details, invoice number and dates, buyer, line items, GST and total, then payment details. Each step below says what to enter and the mistake to avoid.

1. Heading: "Tax Invoice"

Write "Tax Invoice" at the top. The ATO's first required detail is that the document is clearly intended to be a tax invoice, and the heading is the plainest way to show it. Below it, your business name and ABN.

2. Invoice number, date issued and due date

Add the next number in your sequence, the date you issue it, and the due date. The issue date is required on a tax invoice; the due date is what gets you paid on time. "Due 14 days from invoice date" is vaguer than "Due 30 October 2026".

3. Buyer details and the $1,000 rule

For a sale of $1,000 or more, the tax invoice must also show the buyer's identity or ABN. Below that it's optional. A practical rule: if the job is anywhere near $1,000, or the buyer is a business that will claim the GST, add their business name and ABN anyway. It costs nothing and they'll often ask for it.

4. Line items: description, quantity, price

Describe what you supplied clearly enough that the buyer can match it to the job: "Copywriting, 4 hours at $95" beats "Services". Add quantity and price for each line. If any item is GST-free, mark it on the line, because the ATO requires the tax invoice to show the extent to which each sale is taxable.

5. GST: a separate line or "Total price includes GST"

Show the GST as its own line under the subtotal. If every item is taxable and the GST is exactly one-eleventh of the total, you can instead write "Total price includes GST". On a mixed invoice, show the GST figure. GST is 10% of the taxable subtotal, or one-eleventh of a GST-inclusive total; how to calculate GST has the formulas and rounding rules, and the GST calculator checks a single figure.

6. Total

Subtotal plus GST equals the total, and the total is the number the buyer pays. If you took a deposit, show it and the balance due on separate lines so the total still adds up.

7. Payment details

Repeat the due date next to your BSB, account number and PayID, and ask the buyer to use the invoice number as the payment reference. That one line saves most "which invoice was this for?" emails.

8. Send it as a PDF

Export to PDF so the layout and figures can't shift, name the file with the invoice number ("INV-0042 Harbour Lane Cafe.pdf"), and email it. A PDF also prints cleanly for buyers who still pay from paper.

9. Keep the record

Save a copy of every tax invoice you issue. The ATO's general retention period for business records is 5 years, and you'll need the GST figures for your BAS. A folder per financial year, with files named by invoice number, is enough.

A GST-registered Brisbane web designer bills a café for a new website. Prices are quoted GST-exclusive, every item is taxable, and the total is over $1,000, so the buyer's name and ABN go on the invoice.

Common mistakes when making a tax invoice

  • "Tax Invoice" when you're not registered. Only GST-registered businesses issue tax invoices. If you're not, see how to invoice with an ABN.
  • No buyer details on a $1,000+ sale. The most commonly missed field. Add the buyer's name or ABN.
  • GST on GST-free lines. Mark GST-free items and calculate GST on the taxable subtotal only.
  • Taking 10% of a GST-inclusive total. $3,256 includes $296 of GST, not $325.60. Divide by 11.
  • Reusing or skipping invoice numbers. Each invoice gets a unique number, in sequence.
  • Sending an editable file. Send a PDF, and keep your own copy for 5 years.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a tax invoice?

Head the document 'Tax Invoice', add your business name and ABN, an invoice number, the date issued, the buyer's details, each item with quantity and price, the GST (as a line or 'Total price includes GST') and the total. Add payment details, send it as a PDF and keep a copy.

Can I make a tax invoice if I'm not registered for GST?

No. If you're not registered for GST, the ATO says your invoices should not include the words 'tax invoice'. Send a plain invoice headed 'Invoice' with your ABN and no GST.

When does a tax invoice need the buyer's ABN?

For sales of $1,000 or more, the tax invoice must show the buyer's identity or ABN. Below $1,000 it's optional, but it's good practice for business buyers who will claim the GST.

Do I have to give a customer a tax invoice?

If a customer asks for a tax invoice, you must provide one within 28 days, unless the sale was $82.50 (including GST) or less.

How do I make a GST invoice with GST-inclusive prices?

Enter each price including GST and either show the GST as one-eleventh of the total or, if every item is taxable, write 'Total price includes GST'. A $3,256 inclusive total contains $296 of GST.

How long do I keep copies of tax invoices?

The ATO's general retention period for business records is 5 years. Keep a PDF of every tax invoice you issue, filed by invoice number.
