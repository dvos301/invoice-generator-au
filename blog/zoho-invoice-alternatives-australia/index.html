The benchmark you are measuring against. Zoho Invoice is free — Zoho states no cost and no contracts at the time of writing — and it is fully featured: invoicing, a saved client list, stored history, 19-plus languages. It is not perfect: dense interface, front door to a large suite, support aimed at paid-suite customers. But free software that sends and stores invoices sets a high bar.

How we tested: the justify-the-price test. Three questions per tool. What do you get that Zoho Invoice does not already give you for A$0? Is it worth the price at a realistic Australian hourly rate? Does it still produce a valid Australian tax invoice? A tool failing the first question never reaches the other two.

The break-even maths, written out

Here is the formula, because nobody else publishes it: the minutes an app must save each month to pay for itself equals its monthly price divided by your hourly rate, times 60. That is the justify-the-price test in one line.

Those bars are trivially easy to clear on paper. That is the trap, and it is why sole traders end up paying for four subscriptions they never open. Saved admin minutes are not billable minutes — almost nobody sells the fifteen minutes they did not spend retyping an invoice. So set the bar an order of magnitude higher: the app has to save a full hour a month, or do something Zoho Invoice cannot do. At 15 invoices a month that is four minutes an invoice. Four minutes you feel. Fifteen seconds you do not.

Who fails outright. Xero fails for a sole trader: you are buying a general ledger, not invoicing. Invoice Ninja's paid tier fails unless you want your data on your own server. Square's subscription is not the cost — its 2.2% on online card payments is, and on a $5,000 job that is $110, or roughly an hour and a half of billable time, against A$0 on a bank transfer. Three answer it cleanly: InvoiceSonic buys speed, Rounded buys a tax estimate, Bookipi buys recurring billing.

1. InvoiceSonic — Best overall

InvoiceSonic answers the price question with time, not features. It builds a tax invoice in about a minute, sends it, tells you the moment the client opens it, and on Pro chases it if nobody pays. Against a free tool that does the same more slowly, that is the pitch.

The first invoice needs no sign-up and no credit card, so you can time it against your current workflow. Australian handling is built in — ABN field, BSB format, 10% GST label — as part of localised support across supported markets. Clients pay by bank transfer or PayID with no gateway to configure and no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so a $2,400 job arrives as $2,400.

Pros

  • Fastest route from finished job to sent, tracked, automatically chased invoice.
  • Bank transfer and PayID with no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so nothing is shaved off the total.
  • Pro offers monthly and annual billing; check the live USD price before subscribing.

Cons

  • You are paying to leave something free, and the free plan allows only 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month. The 7-day Pro trial requires a card.
  • Not accounting software and not job management: no bank feeds, BAS lodgement, payroll, scheduling or timesheets.

2. Invoice-Generator.com.au — Best free tool

Invoice-Generator.com.au is for people whose objection to Zoho Invoice is the account, not the price. It builds a finished PDF in your browser with no signup, no watermark, no ads and no invoice limit, and the data never leaves the browser.

The Australian invoice template is pre-set with an ABN field, a 10% GST line and a live compliance checklist that flags a missing ABN before you send. There are separate free tools for a quote, a receipt and the GST maths. It has no price to justify, but it is the weakest tool here on capability. It generates a document; Zoho Invoice sends one and remembers the client.

Pros

  • Free forever with no signup, no watermark and no invoice limit.
  • The compliance checklist catches a missing ABN or GST line before your client does.

Cons

  • A PDF only — no sending, no tracking, no automatic reminders, no payment collection.
  • No saved customer list, so repeat clients are re-entered. Zoho Invoice is clearly better here.

Price: free, always. Best for: occasional invoicing and anyone who wants a compliant PDF today without creating another account.

3. Wave — Best free alternative with the books attached

Wave is free in Australia at the time of writing and pairs free invoicing with free accounting. Because it costs nothing, the price test changes shape: what do you gain over Zoho at the same A$0?

Bookkeeping. Zoho Invoice is an invoicing product; Wave puts income and expenses in a ledger beside it, a real step up if you reconcile in a spreadsheet. The trade-off is Australian fit: GST handling is generic rather than ATO-shaped, payments and payroll are built around the US and Canada, and support here is thin. Check what you send against the tax invoice requirements.

Pros

  • Free invoicing and free accounting together, which is more than Zoho Invoice alone gives you.
  • Double-entry books from day one, so nothing is rebuilt at tax time.

Cons

  • GST handling is generic rather than ATO-shaped, so compliance is yours to verify.
  • Australian support is thin and payments and payroll are US and Canada-centric.

Price: free in Australia at the time of writing. Best for: sole traders who want free invoicing and free books and will check their GST wording.

4. Square Invoices — Best for clients who pay by card

Square Invoices gives you unlimited free invoices, estimates and automatic reminders, and the client pays by tapping a card link. You are not paying for software. You are paying for speed of payment.

That makes the price test unusual, because the subscription is the smaller number. Plus is AUD $30 a month, but online card payments cost 2.2%, and 2.2% of a $5,000 job is $110. Bill $60,000 a year all by card and that is $1,320 gone. Worth it only if the card link changes when you get paid: a $5,000 invoice landing in two days instead of thirty means $110 buys 28 days of cash flow. If the client would have bank-transferred anyway, you are donating it.

Pros

  • Unlimited free invoices with automatic reminders — matching Zoho on price, beating it on payment speed.
  • Clients pay from the invoice itself, the most effective way to shorten a payment cycle.

Cons

  • The 2.2% online card fee is the real price: $110 on a $5,000 job, every job.
  • Estimate auto-convert and milestone schedules are Plus only, and there is no job scheduling.

Price: at the time of writing, free, or Plus at AUD $30 per month; 2.2% on online card payments. Best for: businesses whose clients pay faster with a card link, with the 2.2% priced into quotes.

5. Bookipi — Best budget app for recurring billing

Bookipi is a simple invoice app that syncs between web and mobile, and it lists overdue reminders and recurring invoices even on the free plan. If you bill the same clients the same amount monthly, that is a specific answer to the price question.

The catch is volume. Free stops at 3 invoices a month, so the honest read is that Bookipi answers the test on Starter, not on free. Starter is US$9.99 a month and Professional is US$52 a month, both in US dollars, so your cost moves with the Australian dollar — worth noting if you are leaving something free to save money. The break-even still clears easily on recurring billing alone.

Pros

  • Recurring invoices and overdue reminders listed even on the free plan, which is rare at that price.
  • Web and mobile stay in sync, so an invoice started in the ute finishes at the kitchen table.

Cons

  • 3 free invoices a month is far too few for anyone billing regularly.
  • Priced in USD, so the cost of your invoicing moves with the exchange rate.

Price: at the time of writing, free for 3 invoices a month; Starter US$9.99 per month; Professional US$52 per month. Best for: billing a handful of clients on repeat.

6. Invoice Ninja — Best for owning your own data

Invoice Ninja is open source and self-hostable, and that is the reason to consider it. What you buy is not a feature Zoho lacks. It is control over where your invoice history lives.

The free tier caps you at 5 clients, a hard ceiling. Ninja Pro is US$14 a month or US$140 a year at the time of writing. Self-host and the software costs nothing, but you pay in setup — one afternoon configuring a server, then keeping it patched, costs more in billable time than a year of the hosted plan. For most sole traders the maths says take the hosted plan or stay on Zoho.

Pros

  • Open source and self-hostable, so your history cannot be locked behind a plan change.
  • Hosted Pro is one of the cheaper paid options here at US$140 a year at the time of writing.

Cons

  • The free tier stops at 5 clients, and self-hosting is ongoing work, not a one-off.
  • Priced in USD, and Australian GST handling is not stated, so verify it before you send.

Price: at the time of writing, free up to 5 clients; Ninja Pro is US$14 per month or US$140 per year. Best for: operators who want their data on infrastructure they control.

7. Rounded — Best Australian-built option for sole traders

Rounded gives the strongest answer to the price question on this page. Built in Australia for sole traders, it does invoicing, expenses and tax estimates in one place — and the tax estimate is the thing Zoho Invoice does not do.

Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing, which is A$8.25 a month and a break-even of 6.6 minutes at A$75 an hour — the cheapest paid plan here. Pro is $70 a month regular AUD, and promotional pricing varies, so check what year two costs. There is no free plan, so you cannot trial it alongside Zoho, and recurring invoicing is not stated on the pricing page: treat that as unconfirmed and ask first.

Pros

  • Australian-built, so expenses and tax estimates are shaped for the ATO rather than translated.
  • Solo at $99 a year regular AUD clears its break-even on tax admin alone.

Cons

  • No free plan, so there is no way to trial it in parallel for nothing.
  • Recurring invoicing is not listed and promotional pricing varies, so confirm both before switching.

Price: at the time of writing, Solo $99 per year regular and Pro $70 per month regular in AUD; promotional pricing varies. Best for: sole traders who want a running tax estimate beside the invoicing.

8. Xero — Best when your bookkeeper already uses it

Xero is accounting software, not an invoicing app, and that settles the price question. Nobody should leave a free invoicing tool for Xero to get better invoices. You leave for bank feeds, GST and BAS in one system.

For a sole trader sending fifteen invoices a month it fails outright: you are buying a general ledger with an invoicing module inside it, and there is no free plan. With staff and a quarterly BAS the picture flips, because the alternative is paying someone by the hour to re-key invoices between systems. MYOB sits in the same category.

Pros

  • Invoicing, bank reconciliation, GST and BAS in one system your accountant already knows.
  • Australian GST and BAS handling built for the ATO, not adapted from elsewhere.

Cons

  • No free plan, and far more software than a solo operator needs.
  • Overkill as an answer to "Zoho Invoice is free, what else?"

Price: paid plans available — check current pricing. Best for: businesses whose books are already run in Xero by a bookkeeper.

What to look for in Zoho Invoice alternatives

Switching away from something free has a cost even when the new tool is free: you rebuild the client list, change the numbering, and lose the history unless you export it. Only switch for a reason you can name in one sentence.

  • A named reason, not a vague one. "The interface is dense" is a reason. "I want something better" is how people end up on their third invoicing app in a year.
  • The break-even in minutes. Monthly price divided by your hourly rate, times 60. If it cannot plausibly save a full hour a month, it is a hobby purchase.
  • The payment fee, not the subscription. A 2.2% card fee on a $5,000 job is $110, more than any monthly plan here. Bank transfer and PayID cost nothing, as our guide to the best invoice apps in Australia sets out.
  • A real Australian tax invoice. Registered for GST, it must carry the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. The full list is on the ATO site.
  • Whether you need this yet. GST registration is compulsory at A$75,000 of turnover. Below that, a plain invoice with your ABN from a free invoice generator is a complete answer.
  • An export before you migrate. Pull your history out of Zoho Invoice while you still have the account.

Which one should you choose?

  • If Zoho Invoice works and you are only browsing → stay put and spend the hour quoting.
  • If the account and the suite are the problem → the free Australian tax invoice generator, PDF in a minute.
  • If you want speed, tracking and reminders → InvoiceSonic on Pro, billed annually.
  • If you want a tax estimate alongside the invoicing → Rounded Solo, at $99 a year regular AUD.
  • If you want free invoicing plus free books → Wave, with your GST wording checked by hand.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. Stay on Zoho Invoice if it is doing the job. It is free, it sends, it stores your clients and your history, and nothing here beats that when invoicing is already handled — especially with multi-language invoices or the rest of the Zoho suite. A free PDF generator, ours included, is wrong the moment you need sending, tracking, a client list or reminders. InvoiceSonic is wrong if you need bank feeds, BAS lodgement, payroll or job scheduling; that is Xero or MYOB territory. And if you want tax set aside rather than a nicer invoice, Rounded Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing; InvoiceSonic Pro is US$14.99/month on monthly billing. Ranking first here does not make it right for everyone.
