How we tested: the Australian-fit test. Wave is free, which is hard to beat, so the only honest reason to move is fit. Three questions per tool. Is GST handled the way the ATO expects, with the words "Tax invoice", an ABN field and a 10% line? Is the product billed in Australian dollars? And do payments work here — bank transfer, PayID, a card rail that clears into an Australian account?

Who should stay on Wave. If you send a handful of invoices a month, you are under the A$75,000 GST registration threshold, clients pay without being chased, and your accountant is happy with what you hand over, Wave costs nothing and does the job. Free is a real feature: nothing below beats A$0, and three of the eight only match it. Move when the fit starts costing you — a client queries the GST wording, a support ticket sits unanswered, or you export to a spreadsheet to make the books read like Australian books.

The number that decides it. Against a A$0 incumbent every paid alternative has to argue for itself in dollars, so here is the smallest one. InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually is A$168 a year at the time of writing, which is A$14 a month and A$3.23 a week. For that you get unlimited invoices, recurring invoices with automatic reminders, your own logo and colours with no footer, open tracking and online quote acceptance. Whether A$3.23 a week is worth it depends on how much of your week Wave costs you in workarounds. If the answer is nothing, stay.

Who fails the test. Two of the eight fail the Australian-dollar leg outright: Bookipi and Invoice Ninja are priced in US dollars, so your invoicing cost moves with the AUD. Our own free generator fails the payments leg completely — it builds the document and stops. Zoho Invoice, Square, Bookipi and Invoice Ninja did not state ATO-specific GST handling on the pages we checked, the same criticism we make of Wave, so moving for that reason alone may not fix it. Only InvoiceSonic, Rounded, Xero and this site's generator clear the GST leg on published Australian detail.

1. InvoiceSonic — Best overall

InvoiceSonic is the closest like-for-like replacement for Wave's invoicing side that is built for Australia. It clears all three legs of the test: Australian dollar pricing, localised tax handling with a GST label, an ABN field and BSB bank formatting, and payment by bank transfer or PayID with no gateway to set up.

The everyday difference from Wave is what happens after you press send. Pro adds recurring invoices and automatic reminders, invoice open tracking, an email receipt the moment you mark an invoice paid, and online quote acceptance that turns an accepted quote into an invoice in one click. The Australian handling is part of a set of 19 countries, each with the right tax label, tax ID format and bank fields. Be clear about the limit, though: this is invoicing, not accounting. No bank feeds, no BAS lodgement, no payroll — so if you lean on Wave for its books, this replaces half of it.

Pros

  • Priced and billed in Australian dollars, with GST, ABN and BSB fields shaped for Australia.
  • Bank transfer and PayID with no InvoiceSonic transaction fee — the invoice total is what lands.
  • Annual Pro is price-locked, so the rate never rises while the subscription is active.

Cons

  • Not accounting software: no bank feeds, BAS lodgement or payroll, so it replaces Wave's invoicing and not its books.
  • The free plan is capped at 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month, and the 7-day Pro trial needs a card up front.

Price: at the time of writing, the free plan is A$0 and the first invoice needs no sign-up and no credit card; Pro is A$19 a month billed monthly, or A$168 a year billed annually, which is A$14 a month and saves A$60. Best for: Wave users who want the invoicing half done properly in Australian dollars and will keep bookkeeping elsewhere.

3. Zoho Invoice — Best free-for-free swap

Zoho Invoice is free — Zoho states no cost and no contracts — and it is the only tool here that matches Wave feature-for-feature at A$0 while still sending, storing and tracking invoices. If your entire objection to Wave is the North American feel rather than the price, this is the swap that costs nothing.

It publishes an Australian product page and invoices in 19-plus languages, useful if you bill clients who read something other than English. The Australian-dollar leg is moot because there is nothing to pay. What we could not confirm was ATO-specific GST wording, so check the tax settings against the tax invoice requirements before your first send. The other caveat is the surroundings: Zoho Invoice is the front door to a very large suite, the interface is dense next to a single-purpose app, and real support is aimed at paid-suite customers.

Pros

  • Genuinely free with no contract, and it sends, stores and tracks rather than only building a PDF.
  • Multi-language invoicing across 19-plus languages, which no other free option here offers.

Cons

  • The interface is dense, and it is the entry point to a large upsell suite.
  • Support is really built for paid-suite customers, and ATO-specific GST handling is not stated.

Price: free at the time of writing. Best for: Wave users who refuse to start paying for invoicing and want the closest free replacement.

4. Square Invoices — Best if clients pay by card

Square Invoices gives you unlimited free invoices, estimates and automatic reminders, and the client pays by tapping a card link in the email. Where Wave's payment side is built around North America, Square runs an Australian product with Australian dollar pricing, so it clears two legs of the test cleanly.

The catch is that the free plan is not the cheap plan. At 2.2% on online card payments, a business invoicing A$120,000 a year with every client paying by card hands over A$2,640 in fees — fifteen times a A$168 annual subscription collected by bank transfer. Square earns that when money arriving days earlier is worth more than the percentage, which on tight cash flow it often is. Estimate auto-convert and milestone schedules sit on Plus, and ATO-specific GST wording is not stated.

Pros

  • Unlimited free invoices with estimates and automatic reminders on the free plan.
  • Australian dollar pricing and a card rail that often gets the money in the same or next day.

Cons

  • 2.2% on online card payments is A$2,640 a year on A$120,000 of card-paid invoices.
  • Estimate auto-convert and milestone schedules are Plus only, and there is no job scheduling.

Price: at the time of writing, free, or Plus at AUD $30 per month; 2.2% on online card payments. Best for: Wave users whose clients want to tap a card and who will trade a percentage for speed.

5. Bookipi — Best simple app for the phone

Bookipi is a plain invoice app that syncs between web and mobile, and it lists overdue reminders and recurring invoices on every plan, including the free one. For a sole trader whose complaint about Wave is that it feels like accounting software, that simplicity is the appeal.

It fails the Australian-dollar leg. Starter is US$9.99 a month and Professional is US$52 a month at the time of writing, so the cost of your invoicing moves with the exchange rate and never sits still on a budget line. The free plan stops at 3 invoices a month: fine for a side business, useless for anything busier. Australian GST wording is not stated, so it carries the same question mark we put against Wave.

Pros

  • Recurring invoices and overdue reminders even on the free plan, which Wave users usually want next.
  • Web and mobile stay in sync, so an invoice started on the phone finishes on the laptop.

Cons

  • Priced in USD, so your invoicing cost changes with the AUD every month.
  • 3 free invoices a month is very few, and ATO-specific GST handling is not stated.

Price: at the time of writing, free for 3 invoices a month; Starter US$9.99 per month; Professional US$52 per month. Best for: part-time sole traders who want a simple app and can live with USD pricing.

Xero is the Australian answer to that half: bank feeds, GST coding and BAS in one system your accountant already knows. What to look for in a Wave alternative in Australia

Replacing a free tool is a different decision from choosing your first one. You already have a working system, so anything you move to has to fix a specific problem rather than simply exist.

  • Name the thing Wave is costing you. Support you cannot reach, GST wording a client questioned, or a payment method that does not fit. If you cannot name it, the honest answer is to stay.
  • ATO wording, not "tax support". Where you are registered for GST the invoice must carry the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more, per the ATO. Generic tax fields usually get five of those six.
  • The currency the bill arrives in. A US-dollar subscription is a budget line that moves every month. Two of the eight options here are priced that way.
  • How the money actually arrives. Bank transfer and PayID cost nothing and clear overnight; card at 2.2% costs A$2,640 on A$120,000 of card-paid invoices. The payment rail usually costs more than the software.
  • Whether you need the books as well. Wave bundles invoicing and bookkeeping. Most alternatives here do only the first, so check what you are actually replacing before you cancel anything.
  • Whether you need to charge GST at all. Registration is compulsory at A$75,000 of turnover, and rideshare and taxi drivers must register regardless. Under the threshold, a plain invoice with no GST line is fine, which widens your options considerably.

Which one should you choose?

Run the annual cost at the time of writing. Wave is A$0, and so are Zoho Invoice and the free generator on this site. InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually is A$168, or A$3.23 a week. Rounded Solo is $99 a year regular AUD. Bookipi Starter is US$9.99 a month and Invoice Ninja Pro is US$140 a year, both moving with the exchange rate. Square charges nothing up front but 2.2% on card, which is A$2,640 on A$120,000 of card-paid invoices — making the free plan the most expensive option on this page for a busy business.

  • If Wave works and you only wanted to check → stay on Wave, and bookmark this page for the day it stops fitting.
  • If you want Australian shape without paying → the free Australian tax invoice generator, or Zoho Invoice if you also need sending.
  • If you want invoices sent, tracked and chased in A$ → InvoiceSonic on Pro.
  • If you want tax estimates and expenses from an Australian app → Rounded, on Solo.
  • If it was Wave's bookkeeping you relied on → Xero, and budget for it properly.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. If what you valued in Wave was the bookkeeping — bank feeds, categorised transactions, a set of books your accountant can work from — neither InvoiceSonic nor a free PDF generator replaces that, and no amount of good invoicing will. That is accounting software: Xero or MYOB. If you want invoicing plus expenses plus a tax estimate from one Australian product, Rounded's Solo plan is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing, cheaper than InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually, and that is a fair reason to pick it over our number one. And if your only real requirement is a compliant PDF now and then, moving off a free tool to a paid one is a cost with no return — compare the free invoice generators instead and keep the A$168.

What has to be on an Australian tax invoice?

If you are registered for GST the ATO requires the words Tax invoice, your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. Any tool you move to has to produce all six without a workaround.
