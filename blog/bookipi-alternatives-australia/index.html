"Not stated" means exactly that: the tool's own pricing or feature page did not publish the answer at the time of writing, so we have not marked it a yes. How we tested: the AUD-and-sync test. Bookipi is a simple invoice app whose two headline qualities are a low price and syncing between web and mobile. So we asked three questions of every alternative. Does it bill you in Australian dollars, so the number on your statement is the same every month? Does your invoice history sync between phone and web without paying extra for it? And if you stop paying, can you still reach the history?

The worked number. At the time of writing Bookipi is free for 3 invoices a month, then Starter is US$9.99 a month and Professional is US$52 a month. Starter is US$119.88 a year, and what actually leaves your account depends on the rate on the day the payment runs. Across a 0.70 to 0.60 AUD/USD band, that same subscription costs A$171.26, A$184.43 or A$199.80 a year — a A$28.54 spread on a plan that never changed. On Professional it is US$624 a year, which is A$891.43 at 0.70 and A$1,040.00 at 0.60, a swing of A$148.57. Many Australian cards also add an international transaction fee on top, so check your card's terms. InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually is A$168 at the time of writing, and the annual rate is price-locked while the subscription is active. Square Plus is AUD $30 a month at the time of writing, so A$360 a year, also fixed. None of this is a fortune. It is a line in your books you can forecast, which is the entire argument for an AUD-priced tool.

Who fails the test. On currency, two of the eight fall over: Invoice Simple is priced in US dollars at the time of writing, and Billdu does not publish an Australian price on the page we checked. On sync, the free generator on this site fails outright and by design, because it builds a PDF in your browser and saves nothing anywhere — there is no history to sync. On the third question almost everyone fails: InvoiceSonic is the only tool here that states in writing what happens if you downgrade, which is that historical invoices stay accessible and the data is not deleted. Every other tool left that unanswered on the pages we checked. Send their support a one-line email before you move three years of invoices across.

Who should stay on Bookipi. Bookipi puts overdue reminders and recurring invoices on its free plan, and almost nothing else does that at no cost. Square Invoices includes automatic reminders free, but recurring billing across most of this list is either a paid feature or is not listed at all. If you send two or three invoices a month, they go to the same clients each time, and you want them chased automatically for nothing, Bookipi is already doing the job and the USD pricing never touches you because you never pay it. The argument for moving begins the month you go past 3 invoices.

1. InvoiceSonic — Best overall

InvoiceSonic passes both halves of the test: it is priced in Australian dollars, and Pro keeps a secure cloud invoice history rather than a folder of PDFs on one device. That is the straight swap for someone leaving Bookipi because the USD charge annoys them, not because the app annoys them.

The first invoice needs no sign-up and no credit card, which is a fair way to see the output before you commit. On Pro you get unlimited invoices, recurring invoices and automatic reminders, custom branding without a footer, invoice open tracking, and online quote acceptance that converts an accepted quote into an invoice in one click. Australian tax handling is built in — ABN field, BSB format, a 10% GST label — as part of localised support across 19 countries. Clients pay by bank transfer or PayID with no gateway to configure and no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so a $2,400 invoice arrives as $2,400. It is not accounting software: no bank feeds, no BAS lodgement, no payroll.

Pros

  • Priced in AUD, and the annual rate is locked so it does not rise while the subscription is active.
  • Cloud invoice history on Pro, and if you downgrade the historical invoices stay accessible rather than disappearing.
  • Bank transfer and PayID with no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so nothing is shaved off the invoice.

Cons

  • No native Android app. There is an iPhone app; Android users run the web app and add it to the home screen, which is a real step down if the phone is why you were on a mobile invoice app in the first place.
  • The 7-day Pro trial requires a card up front, and the free plan is capped at 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked send and 1 reminder a month.

Price: at the time of writing, the free plan is A$0 and the first invoice needs no sign-up; Pro is A$19 a month billed monthly, or A$168 a year billed annually, which works out at A$14 a month and saves A$60. The 7-day free Pro trial requires a card. Best for: sole traders and small businesses who have outgrown a free plan and want the subscription, the invoice and the history all in Australian dollars.

2. Invoice-Generator.com.au — Best free tool

Invoice-Generator.com.au is a free tax invoice generator that produces a finished PDF in your browser with no signup, no watermark, no ads and no invoice limit. On the currency question it is the cleanest answer on the page, because A$0 does not move with the exchange rate.

The Australian invoice template is pre-set with an ABN field, a 10% GST line and a live compliance checklist that flags a missing ABN before you send. Nothing you type leaves the browser. It fails the sync half of the test completely and we would rather say so plainly: there is no account, no saved client list and no history, so whatever you build is yours to file. Some people want exactly that, particularly if an invoice carries a client's home address. There are separate free tools for a quote, a receipt and the GST maths.

Pros

  • Free forever with no signup, no watermark and no invoice limit — no currency, no renewal, no plan to outgrow.
  • The compliance checklist catches a missing ABN or GST line before the client does.

Cons

  • No sending, tracking, automatic reminders or payment collection, all of which Bookipi does give you.
  • No account and no saved history, so nothing syncs and repeat clients are re-entered each time.

Price: free, always. Best for: anyone who invoices occasionally, or who wants one compliant PDF today without opening another account.

3. Zoho Invoice — Best free account with a real client list

Zoho Invoice is free — Zoho states no cost and no contracts — and unlike a PDF generator it sends the invoice, saves the client and keeps the history in a hosted account. That combination makes it the closest free equivalent to what Bookipi's paid plans give you.

It passes the currency question by not charging you anything, and it passes sync because the history lives on Zoho's servers rather than on one handset. The trade-off is the surroundings. Zoho Invoice is the front door to a very large product suite, the interface is dense next to a single-purpose app, and support is really a paid-suite affair. Recurring invoicing was not listed on the Australian page we checked, which matters if automatic recurring billing is the Bookipi feature you are trying to replace. Nothing is published about what happens to your invoices if you leave, so ask before you build three years of records inside it.

Pros

  • Genuinely free with no contract, and it sends and stores invoices instead of just building a PDF.
  • Multi-language invoicing across 19-plus languages, which is useful if some clients prefer another language.

Cons

  • Dense interface, and it exists to sell you the rest of the Zoho suite.
  • Recurring invoicing is not listed, and support is aimed at paid-suite customers.

Price: free at the time of writing. Best for: people leaving Bookipi who want sending, a client list and a saved history without paying anything in any currency.

4. Square Invoices — Best if clients want to pay by card

Square Invoices gives you unlimited free invoices, estimates and automatic reminders, and the subscription tier is quoted in Australian dollars. The client taps a card link in the email and the money often lands the same day, which is worth real money if cash flow is tight.

It passes the AUD question and the sync question — the account is hosted, so history is reachable from any browser you sign in on. The real cost is not the subscription but the card fee. At 2.2% on online card payments, $60,000 of card-paid invoicing in a year costs $1,320 in fees, which is more than any subscription on this page. Bank transfer and PayID cost nothing by comparison. Estimate auto-convert and milestone payment schedules sit behind Plus, and there is no job scheduling of any kind.

Pros

  • Unlimited free invoices with automatic reminders on the free plan, and Plus is quoted in AUD.
  • Clients pay by card straight from the invoice, so the money usually arrives faster than a bank transfer.

Cons

  • 2.2% on online card payments is $1,320 on $60,000 of card-paid work — the fee, not the plan, is the price.
  • Estimate auto-convert and milestone schedules are Plus only, and there is no scheduling.

Price: at the time of writing, free, or Plus at AUD $30 per month; 2.2% on online card payments. Best for: businesses whose clients will only pay by card and who would rather have the money on Tuesday than save the fee.

5. Invoice Simple — Best like-for-like swap, if you can live with USD

Invoice Simple is the closest thing on this list to Bookipi in spirit: a very simple mobile invoicing app with a small free allowance and cheap paid tiers. If what you like about Bookipi is that it takes 90 seconds and does not ask questions, this is the swap that keeps that feeling.

It fails half the test, though, and it fails the same half Bookipi does. Pricing is in US dollars at the time of writing — Starter from US$7.60 a month, Professional from US$39 a month — so you have swapped one currency-exposed subscription for another, and the annual arithmetic above applies again. GST handling is generic rather than ATO-shaped, which means checking the tax line yourself against the tax invoice requirements rather than trusting the default. We have a fuller comparison of the Invoice Simple alternatives if that is the direction you are heading.

Pros

  • About as simple as mobile invoicing gets, with a free allowance to test it on.
  • Cheap entry price if the exchange rate happens to be in your favour.

Cons

  • Priced in USD, so you keep the exact problem you were leaving Bookipi to solve.
  • Generic GST rather than an Australian tax invoice by default, and cross-device sync is not stated.

Price: at the time of writing, free for 3 invoices a month; Starter from US$7.60 per month; Professional from US$39 per month. Best for: people who want the simplest possible phone invoice and genuinely do not care what currency the bill arrives in.

6. Wave — Best free option with the books attached

Wave is free in Australia at the time of writing, and it bundles basic accounting behind the invoicing rather than only producing documents. Free passes the currency question by default, and the hosted account passes the sync question.

The catch is that Wave is not built for Australia. Support is thin here, GST handling is generic rather than shaped around ATO wording, and the payments and payroll sides of the product are aimed at the United States and Canada. For a sole trader who wants free invoicing with a set of books behind it, that may be an acceptable trade. If you are registered for GST and lodging a BAS, check every tax line before it goes out, and read our rundown of the best free invoice generators in Australia before you settle.

Pros

  • Free in Australia at the time of writing, with accounting attached rather than invoicing alone.
  • Hosted account, so the invoice history is reachable from any browser rather than one phone.

Cons

  • GST handling is generic, not ATO-shaped, so the compliance checking is on you.
  • Australian support is thin and the payments and payroll features are built for North America.

Price: free in Australia at the time of writing. Best for: sole traders who want free invoicing plus simple books and are comfortable checking the tax lines themselves.

7. Rounded — Best Australian-built alternative

Rounded is built in Australia specifically for sole traders and freelancers, and it does invoicing, expenses and tax estimates in one hosted account. It passes both halves of the test without qualification, and it is the only tool here that answers a question Bookipi never tried to: how much tax should you be putting aside.

Price is worth reading carefully, because it is the strongest argument on this page for not choosing our number one. Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing, against A$168 a year for InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually, and Rounded adds expense tracking and a tax estimate for the lower figure. Promotional pricing varies, so check what year two costs. Against that, there is no free plan at all, so there is no way to run a month on it for nothing, and Pro at $70 a month regular AUD is a different conversation entirely.

Pros

  • Australian-built for sole traders, so expenses and tax estimates are shaped for the ATO.
  • Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing — cheaper than most paid plans here, and it does more than invoice.

Cons

  • No free plan, so there is no way to test a real month without paying.
  • Promotional pricing varies and Pro is $70 a month regular AUD, so confirm the renewal price before you switch.

Price: at the time of writing, Solo $99 per year regular and Pro $70 per month regular in AUD; promotional pricing varies. Best for: Australian sole traders who want invoicing, expenses and a tax estimate from one AUD-priced app.

8. Billdu — Best for trying an app before you pay

Billdu is a small-business invoicing app with a free generator on the website and a 7-day free trial on the app itself, after which you move to a paid plan. As a way of testing whether a full invoicing app is worth it, a week costs you nothing.

On our test it is the least transparent of the eight. The pricing page we checked did not publish an Australian price, so we cannot tell you whether your statement will read in AUD or in another currency — and after the exchange-rate arithmetic above, that is not a detail. Cross-device sync and Australian GST handling were not stated either. None of that makes it a bad app; it makes it an app you should price and test yourself before you move a client list into it.

Pros

  • Free invoice generator on the website plus a 7-day free trial of the full app.
  • A genuine small-business invoicing app rather than a document builder.

Cons

  • No Australian price published on the page we checked, so the currency question is unanswered.
  • Free access to the app ends at day 7, and GST handling and sync are not stated.

Price: free generator; the Billdu app has a 7-day free trial then paid plans — check current pricing. Best for: people who want to trial a full invoicing app for a week before committing to anything.

What to look for in a Bookipi alternative

Two things decide this choice, and neither of them is the feature list. What currency the subscription is in, and where your invoice history physically lives. Everything else on the comparison page is negotiable.

  • The currency on your bank statement. A USD subscription is a different number every month. Over a year, US$119.88 lands anywhere between A$171.26 and A$199.80 across a 0.70 to 0.60 band, before any international transaction fee your card adds.
  • Where the history actually lives. A hosted account keeps invoices on a server you can sign into from anything. A phone-only app keeps them on the phone, and phones get dropped, wiped and upgraded.
  • What happens to the history if you stop paying. Ask the question in writing before you switch. InvoiceSonic states that historical invoices stay accessible on downgrade; most tools here say nothing either way.
  • A real Australian tax invoice. If you are registered for GST the invoice must carry the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. That list comes from the ATO, and generic overseas templates routinely miss part of it.
  • How you get paid, and what that costs. PayID and bank transfer clear overnight and cost nothing. Card at 2.2% costs $1,320 on $60,000 of card-paid work, which dwarfs every subscription here.
  • Whether you need an account at all. If you send four invoices a month to four different people, an app is solving a problem you do not have. Our comparison of the best invoice apps in Australia covers where that line sits.

Which one should you choose?

Priced over a full year at the time of writing, the shortlist reads: A$0 with the free generator, Zoho Invoice or Wave; A$168 with InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually; $99 with Rounded Solo; A$360 with Square Plus, plus 2.2% on any card payment; and US$119.88 with Bookipi Starter, which is A$171.26 to A$199.80 depending on the day.

  • If Bookipi's free plan still covers you and the reminders matter → stay where you are, and revisit this when you pass 3 invoices a month.
  • If you have outgrown the free plan and want an AUD price and a history you can reach → InvoiceSonic on Pro.
  • If you want sending, a client list and a saved history for nothing → Zoho Invoice, and accept the dense interface.
  • If your clients will only pay by card → Square Invoices, with the 2.2% written into your prices.
  • If you want expenses and a tax estimate alongside the invoicing → Rounded, at $99 a year for Solo regular AUD.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. If you invoice from an Android handset all day, InvoiceSonic has no native Android app and the web app on the home screen is a compromise — Bookipi's mobile sync may genuinely suit you better, and that is a fair reason to stay. If the number that matters is the annual one, Rounded Solo at $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing is cheaper than InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually and throws in expenses and a tax estimate. If you need bank feeds, BAS lodgement or payroll, none of this is accounting software and you want Xero or MYOB instead. And a free PDF generator, ours included, is the wrong tool the moment you need a saved client, a sent invoice or a reminder — it builds the document and stops there.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best Bookipi alternative in Australia?

InvoiceSonic. It bills in Australian dollars — A$19 a month, or A$168 a year billed annually at the time of writing — and keeps invoice history in a cloud account you can open on a phone or a laptop. Zoho Invoice is the strongest free alternative that still gives you an account, a client list and a saved history.

Is Bookipi priced in Australian dollars?

No. At the time of writing Bookipi is free for 3 invoices a month, then Starter is US$9.99 a month and Professional is US$52 a month. US$9.99 a month is US$119.88 a year, which works out at A$171.26 across a 0.70 exchange band and A$199.80 at 0.60, so the number on your statement moves even though the plan does not.

Does invoice history sync between phone and web on these apps?

On the account-based tools it does, because the history sits on their server rather than on your handset. InvoiceSonic, Zoho Invoice, Square Invoices, Wave and Rounded all work that way. The free generator on this site does not: it builds a PDF in your browser and saves nothing, which is the trade-off for your invoice data never leaving the device.

Is there a free Bookipi alternative that bills in Australian dollars?

Yes. Zoho Invoice and Wave are free in Australia at the time of writing, so there is no foreign currency to convert. The generator on this site is free with no signup and no invoice limit. A free InvoiceSonic account is A$0 too, capped at 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month.

Should I leave Bookipi at all?

Not if the free plan covers you. Bookipi puts overdue reminders and recurring invoices on its free plan, which is unusual at no cost, and USD pricing never touches you while you are paying nothing. The case for moving starts the month you go past 3 invoices and have to choose a paid plan priced in a currency that is not yours.
