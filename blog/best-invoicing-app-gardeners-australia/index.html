"Not listed" means exactly that. The tool's own Australian pricing or feature page did not state that feature at the time of writing, so we have not marked it as a yes. Most "top 10 invoice app" posts assert features nobody checked. If a weekly round or an online quote is the whole reason you are buying software, confirm it on a trial before you commit.

How we tested: the mixed-round test. Three questions per tool. Can it bill a recurring maintenance round automatically, without you opening the app? Can it send a quote for a one-off landscaping job, have it accepted, and turn it into an invoice with a deposit — without retyping the line items? And does the finished document satisfy Australian tax-invoice rules, with an ABN field and a 10% GST line, for both kinds of work?

That test matters because gardening is two businesses in one ute. The maintenance side is high-frequency and low-value: 30 lawns a week at $65 is 1,560 invoices a year, or 130 a month. Even at a brisk two minutes each to open the app, copy last week's invoice, change the date and press send, that is 3,120 minutes — 52 hours of unpaid admin a year. At a conservative 1.5 lawns an hour once travel and the trailer are counted, those 52 hours are 78 lawns you did not mow, or about A$5,070 of billable work. Anything without recurring billing is unusable at that volume. The landscaping side is the mirror image: three or four jobs a year at $4,000 to $12,000, each one won or lost on a quote, each one needing a deposit before you order a pallet of pavers. A tool that serves only one half means two systems and a reconciliation done from memory.

Who fails the test. Only one of the eight publishes both halves as confirmed features on the same plan: InvoiceSonic on Pro, which lists recurring invoices and automatic reminders alongside online quote acceptance and conversion. Bookipi passes the round half — recurring invoices and overdue reminders on every plan, including free — but lists nothing about quotes. Square Invoices sends estimates and automatic reminders on the free plan, but the estimate auto-convert and milestone schedules a staged landscaping job needs sit on Plus. A free PDF generator fails both halves outright: it builds a document, it never sends one. ServiceM8, Zoho Invoice, Rounded and Xero all format an Australian invoice correctly, but none of them stated an automatic recurring send on the pages we checked at the time of writing.

1. InvoiceSonic — Best overall

InvoiceSonic is an invoicing app that builds a professional tax invoice in about a minute, and on Pro it will send the same invoice on a schedule, chase it if nobody pays, and take an accepted quote through to an invoice in one click. For a gardener that combination is the whole job: the Tuesday round bills itself while you are on the mower, and the $6,000 retaining wall gets quoted, accepted online and deposited without you re-keying a line.

It is the only tool here that passes both halves of the mixed-round test. Recurring invoices and automatic reminders are Pro features; so is online quote acceptance and conversion, which most invoicing apps either skip or price separately. You can take a percentage or a fixed deposit up front, and progress claims go out as separate invoices — the right shape for a job billed at deposit, mid-build and completion. Clients pay by bank transfer or PayID with no gateway to set up and no InvoiceSonic transaction fee on those methods, so a $65 mow arrives as $65 and a $6,000 job arrives as $6,000. Invoice open tracking shows the moment the invoice was read, which ends the "never got it" conversation with a property manager. Tax handling is localised across supported markets, which in practice means the ABN field, the BSB format and the 10% GST label are already right rather than adapted. There is an iPhone app; there is no native Android app, so Android users run the web app and add it to the home screen. What it is not is a scheduling tool.

Pros

  • Recurring invoices and automatic reminders on Pro, so a 130-invoice month bills itself.
  • Online quote acceptance and conversion, plus percentage or fixed deposits and progress claims — the landscaping half most invoicing apps ignore.
  • Bank transfer and PayID with no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so nothing is shaved off a $65 mow.

  • Both the recurring round and quote conversion are Pro only. A free account is capped at 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month, which a full round burns through on the first morning.
  • No scheduling, run sheets, job cards, timesheets or staff management, and no materials or cost tracking per job, so the mulch and the pavers still get costed somewhere else.

3. ServiceM8 — Best for scheduling the run, not just billing it

ServiceM8 is Australian-built job management that runs a job from enquiry through to payment, and its free plan is unusual because it includes scheduling. If your real problem is which lawns are on Thursday's run and who is driving, this is the only tool here that solves it at $0.

On the mixed-round test it is a partial pass, and the free cap is the reason. Invoices go out from the job and GST and ABN handling is Australian by default, but neither an automatic recurring send nor an automatic quote-to-invoice conversion was stated on the pricing page at the time of writing, so treat both as unconfirmed and test them on the trial. Then do the arithmetic: 30 lawns a week is 130 jobs a month against a free allowance of 30, so a full round is through the free plan inside the first week. Paid plans are charged per business rather than per user, which is the right shape the moment you put a second person in a second ute — and it is the opposite of how most trade job-management software prices itself.

Pros

  • The free plan includes scheduling, which nothing else in this list offers for nothing.
  • Paid plans are per business, not per user, so a second gardener does not double the bill.
  • Australian-built, so GST and ABN are not an afterthought.

Cons

  • The field app is iPhone and iPad only, so a gardener on an Android handset cannot use it on the run.
  • 30 jobs a month is about one week of a full mowing round, and recurring invoicing is not listed on the pricing page.

Price: at the time of writing, free for 30 jobs a month, then AUD $29, $79, $149 or $349 per month including GST. Best for: gardening businesses with runs and staff to schedule who are already on iPhones.

4. Square Invoices — Best if clients pay by card on the day

Square Invoices gives you unlimited free invoices, estimates and automatic reminders, and the client pays by tapping a card link in the email. For a gardener standing at the gate with a finished job and an owner who will "sort it out later", a card link that clears the same afternoon is worth real money.

It half-passes the mixed-round test. Automatic reminders are on the free plan, but a recurring send is not stated, and estimate auto-convert and milestone payment schedules — exactly what a staged landscaping job needs — are Plus only. The real cost is the card fee, and on gardening invoices it is not a rounding error. At 2.2% on online card payments, a $101,400 mowing round costs $2,230.80 a year in fees, and a single $5,000 landscaping job costs $110. The same money paid by PayID or bank transfer costs nothing. Read it the other way, though, and 2.2% of a $65 mow is $1.43 — cheap insurance against sending three reminders and a text message.

Pros

  • Unlimited free invoices with estimates and automatic reminders on the free plan.
  • Clients pay by card from the invoice itself, so money often lands the same day.

Cons

  • 2.2% on online card payments is $2,230.80 a year on a $101,400 round, and $110 on one $5,000 job.
  • No scheduling, and the estimate auto-convert and milestone schedules a landscaping job needs sit behind the Plus plan.

Price: at the time of writing, free, or Plus at AUD $30 per month; 2.2% on online card payments. Best for: gardeners whose clients will only pay by card and who would rather lose 2.2% than chase the money.

For a weekend gardener with two or three monthly clients, the free plan with recurring billing is genuinely good value at nothing. For a round, it is not a serious option.

Pros

Cons

6. Zoho Invoice — Best free app that actually sends

Zoho Invoice is free — Zoho states no cost and no contracts — and unlike a PDF generator it sends the invoice, saves the client and keeps the history. For a gardener who wants sending and a client list without paying anything at all, nothing else here matches it at A$0.

On the mixed-round test both halves come back unconfirmed: neither an automatic recurring send nor an automatic quote conversion was stated on the Australian page at the time of writing, so check them before you build a 130-invoice month on it. The trade-off is the surroundings. Zoho Invoice sits inside a very large product suite, the interface is dense next to a single-purpose invoicing app, and real support is a paid-suite affair. If you enjoy software, that is fine. If you are invoicing from a phone with one hand while the trailer ramp is down, it is a lot of screen. It does invoice in 19-plus languages, which matters more than you would expect if you bill property managers acting for owners overseas.

Pros

  • Genuinely free with no contract, and it sends and stores invoices rather than just building a PDF.
  • Multi-language invoicing, useful for absentee or overseas owners.

Cons

  • The interface is dense, and it is the front door to a large upsell suite.
  • Support is really aimed at paid-suite customers, and recurring invoicing is not listed.

Price: free at the time of writing. Best for: gardeners who want sending, tracking and a saved client list for A$0 and do not mind a busy interface.

If fuel, blades, mulch and trailer registration matter to you as much as the invoices do, that is a genuine reason to pick it over our number one.

8. Xero — Best if your bookkeeper already uses it

Xero is accounting software, not an invoicing app, and that distinction decides whether it is right for you. It is the correct answer when your bookkeeper already works in Xero and wants invoices, bank feeds, GST and BAS in one system instead of three.

For a one-ute gardener it is more software than the job needs, and it fails the spirit of the mixed-round test: you are buying a general ledger, and the invoicing is one module inside it. There is no free plan. The picture flips at scale, though. A round turning over $101,400 is registered for GST, lodging a BAS quarterly and probably paying someone to keep the books, and at that point paying twice — once for invoicing, once for bookkeeping, then paying again for the re-keying between them — costs more than one system your accountant can already read. If somebody else does your books, ask them what they want before you choose anything on this page.

Pros

  • Bank feeds mean 130 small deposits reconcile themselves instead of being ticked off a statement by hand.

Cons

  • No free plan, and no recurring-invoice or quote-conversion claim we could verify on the Australian pricing page.
  • Considerably more software than a solo gardener needs.

Price: paid plans available — check current pricing. Best for: gardening and landscaping businesses whose books are already run in Xero by a bookkeeper.

What to look for in an invoicing app for gardeners

Gardening bills in two rhythms at once: 130 small invoices a month off the maintenance round, and three or four large quoted jobs a year off the landscaping side. An app that serves one rhythm forces you to keep a second system for the other. These six things decide it.

  • Recurring invoicing, not a saved template. A saved template still needs you to open the app, change the date and press send 1,560 times a year. A recurring invoice goes out whether you remember or not, and it keeps going out the week you are off with a bad back.
  • Quote acceptance that converts. A landscaping job is won on the quote. Being able to send it, have the client accept it online, and turn it into an invoice without retyping the line items is the whole difference between one system and two. Our free quote generator covers the document if your app does not do the conversion.
  • Deposits and progress claims. You should not be buying $2,400 of pavers with your own money. Look for a percentage or fixed deposit taken up front and the ability to raise progress claims as separate invoices at each stage, rather than one invoice at the end and a fortnight of hoping.
  • Automatic reminders. On 130 invoices a month, even a 5% late rate is six or seven chases every month. Reminders on the due date and again a week later recover most of them without a phone call; our guide to chasing overdue invoices in Australia covers what to do when they do not.
  • A proper Australian tax invoice, and whether you need GST at all. Registration is compulsory at A$75,000 of turnover: 22 lawns a week at $65 is $74,360 and sits under it, 23 lawns is $77,740 and does not. Check the GST registration threshold before you switch GST on in any app, then make sure the invoice carries the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of the work with quantity and price, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more — a rule that catches landscaping jobs but not mows. The full list of tax invoice requirements in Australia matches the ATO guidance.
  • The payment method and the fee attached to it. PayID and bank transfer cost nothing and clear overnight. Card payments at around 2.2% cost $2,230.80 a year on a $101,400 round and $110 on a single $5,000 job. On invoices as small as a mow, the payment fee is bigger than the software.

Which one should you choose?

  • If you mow six or eight lawns a week and send a few invoices a month → the free Australian tax invoice generator, and email the PDF.
  • If you run a full maintenance round and quote landscaping jobs on top of it → InvoiceSonic on Pro, so the round bills itself and the quotes convert.
  • If you want sending and a saved client list for nothing → Zoho Invoice, and accept the dense interface.
  • If you are scheduling two utes and a run sheet → ServiceM8, starting on the free 30-job plan and expecting to pass it inside the first week.
  • If the tax and the expenses worry you more than the invoicing does → Rounded, at $99 a year regular for Solo at the time of writing.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. Once you are scheduling two utes, a run sheet and a casual on Saturdays, an invoicing app is the wrong tool: ServiceM8 puts scheduling on its free plan and prices paid plans per business rather than per user, and InvoiceSonic does no scheduling, job cards, timesheets or staff management at all. It also does no materials or cost tracking, so if you need to know what the pavers, mulch and plants cost against a $6,000 job to work out whether you made money on it, that is job-management software, not invoicing software. If tax and expenses are the part that keeps you up, Rounded is Australian-built for sole traders and Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing —; InvoiceSonic Pro is US$14.99/month on monthly billing. Rounded adds expense tracking on top. And once you have staff, a bookkeeper and a quarterly BAS on $101,400 of turnover, you are in accounting-software territory: Xero or MYOB, not an invoicing app. A free PDF generator, ours included, is the wrong choice the moment the same invoice has to go out more than once — which for a mowing round is day two.

Can one app handle both a weekly mowing round and a quoted landscaping job?

Do gardeners have to charge GST on their invoices?

Only once you are registered, which is compulsory at A$75,000 of turnover. Thirty lawns a week at $65 is $101,400 a year, well over the line, so a full-time mowing round has to be registered and charge 10% GST. Twenty-two lawns a week is $74,360 and sits under it, so the 23rd weekly lawn at that price is the one that tips you over.

Is there a genuinely free invoicing app for gardeners?

Yes. Invoice-Generator.com.au is free with no signup, no watermark and no invoice limit, and Zoho Invoice is free at the time of writing. Square Invoices has a free plan too, but 2.2% comes off every online card payment at the time of writing.

What has to be on a gardener's tax invoice in Australia?

If you are registered for GST the ATO requires the words Tax invoice, your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied including quantity and price, the GST amount or a statement that the total includes GST, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. That last rule catches landscaping jobs, not mows.
