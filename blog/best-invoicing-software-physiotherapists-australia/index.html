How we tested: the funding-mix test. Three questions per tool. Can it apply a different tax treatment to different invoices, so one carries 10% GST and the next is GST-free? Can it store the reference numbers each funding source asks for — a participant's NDIS number and support item number, an insurer's claim number, a scheme's referral reference — without retyping? And can it bill somebody who is not the patient — the plan manager, the insurer or the department pays, not the person on the table?

The worked number. Most NDIS supports are GST-free where they are a reasonable and necessary support in a participant's plan, so an NDIS invoice often shows no GST even when the provider is registered, while other work may be invoiced with GST included. Whether a service is GST-free depends on the service and the funding source, so settle that with the ATO or your accountant, not an app's default. Now the arithmetic. A $95 consult invoiced GST-inclusive contains $8.64 of GST ($95 ÷ 11). If 20 of your 60 invoices a month carry a GST line they should not, that is $172.73 a month and $518.18 a quarter to unwind as a BAS correction — every quarter, until somebody notices. The GST calculator does that sum for any fee.

Who fails the test. None of the eight passes all three questions outright. InvoiceSonic is the only tool here publishing a dedicated NDIS invoice template, which removes the reference-number problem for the stream most likely to be queried. A free PDF generator passes the first two by brute force — type any tax line and any reference you like — and fails the third, because nothing is stored. The other six send properly formatted invoices, but none stated a funding-scheme template or reference field on the pages we checked at the time of writing. Wave is the weakest fit: generic GST handling is the wrong start for a week mixing GST-free and taxable work.

What to look for in invoicing software for physiotherapists

Physio billing is not high-volume, it is high-variety: one 40-minute appointment can be invoiced four ways depending on who pays. Flexibility per invoice matters more than the feature list, and tax treatment matters more than the subscription.

  • Per-invoice tax treatment, not one global switch. You need a GST-inclusive invoice and a GST-free one from the same account in the same week. What applies to a service depends on the service and the funding source — settle that with the ATO or your accountant first.
  • Somewhere to put the scheme's reference numbers. An NDIS invoice needs the participant's name and NDIS number, the support item number from the NDIS Price Guide or Support Catalogue, the service dates and hours, and your ABN. DVA and workers-compensation invoices carry their own — check the requirements with the scheme or insurer.
  • A bill-to party who is not the patient. Plan-managed, self-managed and NDIA-managed participants are billed differently, and NDIA-managed claims go through the myplace provider portal. If a tool assumes the person treated is the person paying, half your invoices need a workaround.
  • A proper Australian tax invoice. Where you are registered for GST it must carry the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. The full list of tax invoice requirements is on the ATO site.
  • Records that outlast the subscription. Health billing gets queried well after the fact. InvoiceSonic keeps historical invoices accessible after a downgrade; a browser-based generator keeps nothing, so save your own copies.
  • Whether your problem is invoicing or practice management. One physio invoicing between patients has an invoicing problem. A clinic with a diary, clinical notes and claiming has a practice-management problem, and nothing here solves it. Our allied health guide draws the same line.
