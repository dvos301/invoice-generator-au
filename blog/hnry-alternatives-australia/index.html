How we tested: the 1% test. Three questions per tool. What does Hnry's 1% plus GST come to at your income? What would this tool cost for the same year, as one flat number? And what work lands back on you once nobody is paying the tax for you?

Start with what Hnry charges. At the time of writing it takes 1% plus GST of every payment into your Hnry account, capped at $1,500 plus GST a year, with a minimum of $0.50 plus GST on payments under $50. Including the GST that is 1.1% of your income. In return it receives the money, pays your income tax and GST, and passes on the rest. That is genuine work, and none of the eight tools below does all of it. If you are registered for GST, ask your accountant whether the GST portion comes back as an input tax credit.

3. Rounded — Best for invoicing, expenses and a tax estimate

Rounded is Australian-built for sole traders and freelancers, covering invoicing, expenses and tax estimates in one place. It is the cheapest paid option here: Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing.

On the 1% test it beats Hnry at almost every income: $99 against A$660 at A$60,000, and against A$1,320 at A$120,000. Even Pro at $70 a month regular, or $840 a year, undercuts Hnry above about A$76,000. The limit is question three — Rounded estimates your tax, it does not pay it.

Pros

  • Built in Australia, so expenses, GST and tax estimates are shaped for the ATO.
  • Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing, the lowest flat price here.

Cons

  • No free plan, so there is no way to run a quarter on it before committing.
  • It estimates tax rather than paying it, and promotional pricing varies, so check year two.

Price: at the time of writing, Solo $99 per year regular and Pro $70 per month regular in AUD; promotional pricing varies. Best for: sole traders who want the tax number in front of them without paying a percentage.

4. Thriday — Best for automated bookkeeping and BAS

Thriday is the closest thing here to what Hnry does: a business transaction account with invoicing, automated bookkeeping and BAS, so the money and the paperwork sit in one place.

It is free under A$50,000 of revenue at the time of writing, and Standard is A$16.50 a month billed annually, or A$198 a year. At A$120,000 that is A$198 against Hnry's A$1,320 — A$1,122 a year — and it is still automating the books and the BAS. The catch is the one you would expect from a product built around an account: you have to bank with Thriday.

Pros

  • Automated bookkeeping and BAS alongside the invoicing, the part most flat-price tools leave out.
  • Free under A$50,000 of revenue at the time of writing, so a side business runs on it for nothing.

Cons

  • You must move your business banking to Thriday to get the benefit — a real switching cost.
  • It automates the paperwork but does not pay the tax for you the way Hnry does.

Price: at the time of writing, free under A$50,000 revenue; Standard A$16.50 a month billed annually. Best for: sole traders who liked Hnry's hands-off admin and will change banks for it.

5. Solo App — Best for phone-first sole traders

Solo App is another Australian product for sole traders, covering invoicing, expenses and tax in one app made for a phone. From $24 a month AUD at the time of writing, that is from A$288 a year.

It clears the 1% test comfortably — A$288 against A$1,320 at A$120,000 — but it is the dearest of the three Australian flat-price options, close to three times Rounded Solo. It earns the difference on habit: if you went to Hnry because admin never got done, an app you will open on site beats a cheaper one you will not.

Pros

  • Australian, with invoicing, expenses and tax in one phone-first app.
  • Flat monthly pricing, so the cost stops tracking your income.

Cons

  • No free plan at all, so there is no trial quarter to test the habit.
  • From A$288 a year is close to three times Rounded Solo at the time of writing.

Price: from $24 per month AUD at the time of writing. Best for: sole traders who do their admin on a phone and want tax kept alongside it.

6. Zoho Invoice — Best free tool that actually sends

Zoho Invoice is free — Zoho states no cost and no contracts — and unlike a PDF generator it sends the invoice, saves the client and keeps the history. For the invoicing half, it is the strongest A$0 option.

On the test it scores A$0 at every income level, which no percentage-based product can match. The trade-offs are the surroundings: it sits inside a very large product suite, the interface is dense next to a single-purpose app, support is aimed at paid-suite customers, and it does nothing about tax.

Pros

  • Genuinely free with no contract, and it sends and stores invoices rather than only building one.
  • Multi-language invoicing across 19-plus languages, useful if you bill overseas clients.

Cons

  • The interface is dense, and it is the front door to a large upsell suite.
  • Support is aimed at paid-suite customers, and there is no tax or BAS help.

Price: free at the time of writing. Best for: sole traders who want sending, tracking and a client list for nothing.

7. Xero — Best when an accountant does your tax

Xero is accounting software rather than an invoicing app, and for a certain kind of ex-Hnry user that is exactly right. Bank feeds, GST tracking and BAS live in one system your accountant already works in.

It cannot be scored on the 1% test, because Xero published no Australian price we could quote at the time of writing. The shape is clear: a flat subscription, not a percentage, and best read as the software half of an arrangement where a bookkeeper does the work Hnry was doing. That usually costs more than A$1,320 a year, so it is not a way to switch on price.

Pros

  • Invoicing, bank reconciliation, GST and BAS in one system accountants already know.
  • A flat subscription, so the price does not climb with a good year.

Cons

  • No free plan, and no Australian price we could verify at the time of writing.
  • Far more software than a solo operator needs, and it replaces Hnry's tax work only if you pay someone to run it.

Price: paid plans available — check current pricing. Best for: sole traders with a bookkeeper, or a business that has outgrown a solo tax service.

8. Square Invoices — Best when clients will only pay by card

Square Invoices gives you unlimited free invoices, estimates and automatic reminders, and the client pays by tapping a card link. If clients take three weeks to do a bank transfer, that is worth real money.

It is also the one tool here that fails the 1% test worse than Hnry. At the time of writing online card payments cost 2.2%, a percentage of income with no annual ceiling: A$110 on a $5,000 job, and A$2,640 on A$120,000 of billings if every client pays by card, where Hnry stops at A$1,650. Square is only cheap if the card link is the exception.

Pros

  • Unlimited free invoices with estimates and automatic reminders, at no cost.
  • Clients pay from the invoice itself, so money often lands same day.

Cons

  • 2.2% on online card payments is uncapped — twice Hnry's rate at A$120,000, and it keeps climbing past where Hnry stops.
  • Estimate auto-convert and milestone payment schedules sit behind Plus at AUD $30 a month.

Price: at the time of writing, free, or Plus at AUD $30 per month; 2.2% on online card payments. Best for: sole traders whose clients insist on card and who price the 2.2% in.

What to look for in Hnry alternatives

Leaving a percentage-based service is two decisions: which tool sends the invoice, and who does the tax. Get the second wrong and the saving disappears into penalties and a bad June.

  • InvoiceSonic Pro is listed at US$14.99/month on monthly billing, in USD. Check the current exchange rate and plan details.
  • Who lodges the BAS. Registered for GST means lodging quarterly, and registration is compulsory at A$75,000 of turnover — read the GST registration threshold rules first. Rideshare and taxi drivers must register regardless of turnover.
  • Whether you have to change banks. Thriday's economics depend on banking with Thriday: fine when starting out, painful when clients already pay a BSB you have used for years.
  • InvoiceSonic Pro is listed at US$14.99/month on monthly billing, in USD. Check the current exchange rate and plan details.
  • A tax invoice that still meets the ATO's rules. Registered for GST, it must carry the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. Invoice without an ABN and the payer may have to withhold 47% — see whether you need an ABN and the ATO site.
  • Somewhere to put the money you now set aside. Hnry's real product is that the tax never sits in your account looking spendable. Replace it with a second account and a standing rule — the GST calculator gives the 10%, and how to invoice as a sole trader covers the rest.

Which one should you choose?

  • If your income through Hnry is under about A$15,300 a year → stay on Hnry: 1% plus GST is less than any paid subscription here, and it does your tax.
  • If you bill A$60,000 to A$150,000 and will lodge your own BAS → InvoiceSonic on Pro, keeping the A$492 to A$1,482 difference.
  • If you want bookkeeping and BAS automated and will move banks → Thriday, A$198 a year on Standard at the time of writing.
  • If you want the cheapest Australian paid app with a tax estimate → Rounded Solo, $99 a year regular AUD.
  • If you send a handful of invoices a year → the free sole trader invoice generator, putting tax aside yourself.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. Neither does anything about tax, and tax is why people are on Hnry. If you will not set money aside, lodge a BAS on time or pay an accountant to, Hnry's 1% is buying something real, and A$1,152 a year is a fair price for never thinking about it. If you want most of that automation back at a flat price, Thriday is the closer swap, provided you bank with them. If your books need feeds and a bookkeeper, that is Xero or MYOB territory. And for the cheapest flat-price tool with a tax estimate, Rounded Solo undercuts InvoiceSonic Pro. A free PDF generator, ours included, is wrong the moment sending matters more than the document.

Frequently asked questions

What does Hnry cost in Australia?

At the time of writing Hnry charges 1% plus GST of every payment into your Hnry account, capped at $1,500 plus GST a year, with a minimum of $0.50 plus GST on payments under $50. Including GST that is 1.1% of income: A$660 on A$60,000 and A$1,320 on A$120,000.

What is the best alternative to Hnry in Australia?

InvoiceSonic currently lists Pro at US$14.99/month on monthly billing, in USD. Compare today’s exchange rate and the provider’s live pricing before choosing.

Is Hnry's 1% fee capped?

Yes. At the time of writing the fee is capped at $1,500 plus GST a year. Because 1% of A$150,000 is $1,500, the cap bites at A$150,000 of payments in. Above that the rate falls: at A$200,000 the same A$1,650 including GST is 0.83%.

Is there a free alternative to Hnry?

For the invoicing half, yes. Invoice-Generator.com.au is free with no signup, no watermark and no invoice limit, and Zoho Invoice is free at the time of writing. Neither does the tax half, so a free tool only replaces Hnry if you lodge your own BAS and set money aside.

What do I have to do myself if I leave Hnry?

The work Hnry was doing comes back to you or your accountant: setting money aside for income tax, PAYG instalments if the ATO has issued them, and a quarterly BAS if you are registered for GST. Registration is compulsory at A$75,000 of turnover. Check your circumstances with your accountant.
