How we tested: the Australian-fit test. Three questions per tool. Does it speak Australian tax — GST rather than sales tax, an ABN field rather than an EIN field, a BSB rather than a routing number? Is it billed in Australian dollars, at a number that will be the same next month? And does it cover the Australian obligation you actually have, which for most sole traders is a compliant tax invoice and for a GST-registered business with staff is a quarterly BAS?

Australian fit is not a branding question. It is the difference between an invoice that satisfies the ATO and one that does not. A tax invoice from a GST-registered business must carry the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. If the software calls that field "Tax ID" and leaves the wording to you, you are doing compliance by hand inside software you are paying for. Our full breakdown of tax invoice requirements lists every field.

The worked number: what USD pricing actually costs you. Every tool in this list is either free or billed in Australian dollars, and that was a deliberate exclusion rule rather than an accident. Take a plan advertised at US$23 a month. Convert at 65 Australian cents to the US dollar and that is A$35.38 a month, or A$424.62 a year. Convert at 60 cents and the identical software is A$38.33 a month, or A$460.00 a year. That is A$35.38 a year of difference in the same subscription, decided by a currency market you have no view on, landing on your statement as a slightly different number every month and a small foreign-transaction fee on top. By contrast, InvoiceSonic annual Pro is A$168 a year at the time of writing and is price-locked, so the rate never rises while the subscription is active — A$168 this year is A$168 next year. Those exchange rates are illustrative, not today's rate; the point is the spread, and the spread is the argument.

Who fails the test. Wave fails hardest: it is free in Australia at the time of writing, but its GST handling is generic rather than ATO-shaped, its Australian support is thin, and its payments and payroll are built for the United States and Canada. Zoho Invoice half-fails — it invoices well and costs nothing, but it is a global product with an Australian page, not an Australian product, and it does no BAS. A free PDF generator, ours included, fails the third question outright: it produces a compliant document and nothing else. And a long list of popular apps never made this article because they are priced in US dollars — FreshBooks at US$23, US$43 and US$70 a month, Bookipi Starter at US$9.99, Invoice2go Pro at US$19, Invoice Ninja Pro at US$14, all at the time of writing. Good software, wrong currency for an Australian bank statement.

Who should stay on QuickBooks. Be honest with yourself before you migrate. QuickBooks is capable accounting software with real Australian GST and BAS support, and there are four situations where leaving it is a downgrade dressed up as a saving. Stay if you run payroll through it. Stay if you track inventory in it. Stay if you have years of reconciled bank-feed history that a new tool cannot inherit and you would have to rebuild by hand. And stay if your accountant works in it, because a cheaper subscription that adds three hours to their bill is not cheaper. What most people searching for an alternative actually want is something simpler or cheaper, and very often they only need invoicing — in which case they have been paying for a general ledger they never opened.

1. InvoiceSonic — Best overall

InvoiceSonic is the strongest QuickBooks replacement for anyone whose real use of QuickBooks was the invoice screen. It builds a professional tax invoice in about a minute, sends it, tracks whether it was opened, and chases it if nobody pays — which is the part of accounting software most sole traders use and almost the only part.

On the Australian-fit test it passes the first two questions cleanly. Tax is labelled GST, the tax ID field takes an ABN, the bank field takes a BSB, and the currency is Australian, all as part of localised support across 19 countries with GST, VAT and Sales Tax labelling and ABN, VAT Number and EIN formats. Pricing is quoted in Australian dollars, and the annual plan is price-locked. Clients pay by bank transfer or PayID with no gateway to configure and no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, because the money moves bank to bank. Quotes convert to invoices in one click on Pro, and you can take a percentage or fixed deposit up front. It fails the third question by design: this is not accounting software, so there are no bank feeds, no BAS lodgement and no payroll.

Pros

  • GST, ABN, BSB and AUD are native, not a "Tax ID" box you fill in yourself.
  • Annual Pro is A$168 at the time of writing and price-locked, so the number does not move.
  • PayID and bank transfer with no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so a $2,400 invoice arrives as $2,400.

Cons

  • Not accounting software: no bank feeds, no BAS lodgement, no payroll. If that is why you were on QuickBooks, this is a step backwards.
  • The free plan is capped at 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month, and the 7-day Pro trial requires a card up front.

Price: at the time of writing, the free plan is A$0 and the first invoice needs no sign-up and no credit card; Pro is A$19 a month billed monthly, or A$168 a year billed annually, which works out at A$14 a month and saves A$60. Best for: sole traders and small businesses who only ever used QuickBooks to invoice, and want that part done properly in Australian dollars.

3. Xero — Best when the bookkeeper already uses it

Xero is accounting software rather than an invoicing app, and that is the whole point of it as a QuickBooks alternative: it is the closest like-for-like swap, with bank feeds, GST and BAS in one system. If you are leaving QuickBooks but genuinely need the accounting, this is the switch that loses you nothing.

It passes the Australian-fit test on compliance and fails it on proportion. GST and BAS are handled properly and your bookkeeper almost certainly knows it already, which is worth more than any feature list. But there is no free plan, the Australian price was not stated in a form we could quote at the time of writing, and for a solo operator sending a dozen invoices a month it is a general ledger with an invoicing module bolted inside. The migration is also not free in hours. If you are a sole trader wondering whether you need this much software, our guide to Xero alternatives for sole traders works through the same question from the other direction.

Pros

  • Bank feeds, GST and BAS in one system, so nothing gets re-keyed at quarter end.
  • Your accountant or bookkeeper likely works in it already, which cuts their hours rather than yours.

Cons

  • No free plan and no trial-and-forget option, so the decision has to be made before you have used it in anger.
  • Considerably more software than a solo operator needs, and you pay for the parts you never open.

Price: paid plans available — check current pricing. Best for: GST-registered businesses with a bookkeeper, staff or a BAS they lodge from their own books.

4. MYOB — Best like-for-like Australian incumbent

MYOB is the Australian accounting incumbent and the most conservative move on this list. If your objection to QuickBooks is that it is a global product with Australia bolted on, MYOB is the answer that inverts that: an Australian accounting package that has been shaped around Australian small business for decades.

On the first test question it does well, which is exactly what you are buying. On the second and third it needs checking: the Australian price was not stated in a form we could quote at the time of writing, and BAS handling is not something we found stated on the pages we checked, so confirm it against your own plan before you commit. The honest reservation is weight. MYOB is heavier than a solo trader needs, and swapping one full accounting suite for another full accounting suite solves a preference, not a workload. If QuickBooks felt like too much software, MYOB will too.

Pros

  • Australian accounting incumbent, shaped for Australian small business rather than localised into it.
  • The most like-for-like swap available if you need to keep full accounting and just want to leave Intuit.

Cons

  • No free plan, and heavier than a sole trader needs — the same complaint most people have about QuickBooks.
  • Pricing was not quotable at the time of writing, so budget it from their site before you migrate.

Price: paid plans available — check current pricing. Best for: established Australian businesses that need full accounting and want an Australian vendor.

5. Rounded — Best for sole traders who want a tax estimate

Rounded is Australian-built specifically for sole traders and freelancers, and it does invoicing, expenses and tax estimates in one place. For someone who has never set money aside for tax, that estimate is the feature that justifies the subscription on its own.

It passes the first two questions well: Australian-built, priced in Australian dollars, and shaped for the way a sole trader's tax actually works. On the third it stops short of full accounting — expenses and a tax estimate are not BAS lodgement, and BAS was not stated on the pages we checked. There is no free plan, so you cannot run a quiet month on it for nothing. Note the price honestly, though, because it is a real reason to pick Rounded over our number one: Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing, which is less than InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually at A$168, and it tracks your expenses and estimates your tax as well. Promotional pricing varies, so check what year two costs.

Pros

  • Australian-built for sole traders, so expenses and tax estimates are shaped for the ATO rather than the IRS.
  • Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing — cheaper than most paid options here, and it does more than invoice.

Cons

  • No free plan at all, so there is no zero-cost way to trial it against your real invoices.
  • Not full accounting: BAS was not listed, and promotional pricing varies, so the second year may not match the first.

Price: at the time of writing, Solo $99 per year regular and Pro $70 per month regular in AUD; promotional pricing varies. Best for: freelancers and sole traders who want invoicing, expenses and a tax estimate from one Australian app.

6. Thriday — Best if you want banking, bookkeeping and BAS together

Thriday is the most structurally different option here: an Australian business transaction account with invoicing, automated bookkeeping and BAS built on top of it. Because the money and the books are the same system, most of the categorising and reconciling that fills a QuickBooks evening simply does not need doing.

It passes all three test questions, which nothing else on this list does at A$0. It is Australian, it is priced in Australian dollars, and it covers BAS. It is free under A$50,000 of revenue at the time of writing, and Standard is A$16.50 a month billed annually — for a business turning over $40,000 that is a genuine zero-cost replacement for paid accounting software. The catch is the condition attached: you have to bank with Thriday to get the value. Moving your business banking is a bigger decision than changing invoicing software, and it is not reversible on a Tuesday afternoon.

Pros

  • Free under A$50,000 revenue at the time of writing, with invoicing, bookkeeping and BAS included.
  • Australian, priced in AUD, and the transaction account feeds the books directly instead of via a bank feed.

Cons

  • You must bank with Thriday to get the benefit, which means changing where your business money lives.
  • Over A$50,000 of revenue you are on Standard at A$16.50 a month billed annually, at the time of writing.

Price: at the time of writing, free under A$50,000 revenue; Standard A$16.50 a month billed annually. Best for: newer Australian businesses willing to move their banking to get bookkeeping and BAS handled for nothing.

Wave offers free invoicing and free accounting, and it is free in Australia at the time of writing. On paper that makes it the boldest QuickBooks alternative on this list: accounting software at no cost. Its GST handling is generic rather than ATO-shaped, so the compliance detail that matters on an Australian tax invoice is left to you. Australian support is thin. Payments and payroll are built for the United States and Canada, which means the two features that would justify accounting software are the two least useful to you here. If you are a hobby business, or you invoice rarely and your accountant fixes everything at year end, free accounting is still free accounting. Pros

Cons

  • Payments and payroll are US and Canada centric, so the accounting features you would want are the ones that do not apply.

Price: free in Australia at the time of writing. Best for: low-volume or pre-GST businesses who want free accounting and will do the Australian compliance detail themselves.

What to look for in QuickBooks alternatives in Australia

The mistake that put most people on QuickBooks is buying accounting software to solve an invoicing problem. Work out which one you have first, then check the six things below in order — the currency and the tax labels matter more than the feature grid.

  • GST, not sales tax. The label is a proxy for everything behind it. Software that says "Sales Tax" and lets you rename the field is software where the 10% GST line, the words "Tax invoice" and the rounding are your responsibility.
  • An ABN field, not a Tax ID box. No ABN on an invoice can mean the payer is required to withhold 47% of the payment, so this is not cosmetic. Check the tax invoice requirements against the fields the software actually gives you.
  • BSB and account number, not a routing number. If the bank details block is a free-text field you type into by hand, expect a client to pay the wrong account eventually. PayID support is worth more than a card gateway for most Australian B2B work.
  • Billed in Australian dollars. A US$23 plan is A$424.62 a year at 65 cents and A$460.00 at 60 cents. That is A$35.38 of variance you cannot forecast, plus a foreign-transaction fee, for identical software.
  • Whether you need BAS at all. GST registration is compulsory at A$75,000 of turnover ($150,000 for not-for-profits), and taxi and ride-sourcing drivers must register regardless of turnover. Under the threshold and unregistered, most of what accounting software sells you is unused.
  • eInvoicing, if you bill government or large businesses. Australian eInvoicing runs on the Peppol network, overseen by the ATO. If your clients are asking for it, that narrows the field faster than price does.

Which one should you choose?

Run the annual cost in Australian dollars, at the time of writing. Invoice-Generator.com.au, Zoho Invoice and Wave are A$0. Thriday is A$0 under A$50,000 of revenue, then A$198 a year on Standard billed annually. Rounded Solo is $99 a year regular AUD. InvoiceSonic Pro is A$168 a year billed annually, price-locked. Xero, MYOB and QuickBooks itself are paid plans with pricing to check on their own sites. Notice what that list does not contain: a single figure that moves with the exchange rate.

  • If you only ever used QuickBooks to raise invoices → InvoiceSonic, on Pro if you want recurring invoices and reminders.
  • If you want to cancel everything today and still invoice legally → the free Australian tax invoice generator, and email the PDF.
  • If you need bank feeds, payroll and BAS in one system → Xero, or MYOB if you want an Australian vendor.
  • If you are under A$50,000 of revenue and will move your banking → Thriday, and get bookkeeping and BAS for nothing.
  • If you want invoicing plus expenses and a tax estimate for under A$100 a year → Rounded Solo.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. Neither is accounting software, and pretending otherwise costs more than a subscription. InvoiceSonic has no bank feeds, no BAS lodgement and no payroll, and a free PDF generator has no sending, tracking, reminders or saved customer list. If you run staff on payroll, carry inventory, or lodge a quarterly BAS from your own books, the right answer is Xero, MYOB or staying on QuickBooks — and if you have years of reconciled bank-feed history in QuickBooks, staying is very likely cheaper than rebuilding it. If your accountant works in one specific package and bills you by the hour, use that package: the software is not the expensive part. And if what you want is tax handled rather than tracked, Rounded's Solo plan at $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing does invoicing, expenses and a tax estimate for less than InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually, which is a fair reason to pick it over our number one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best QuickBooks alternative in Australia?

For most people it is InvoiceSonic, because most people leaving QuickBooks want invoicing rather than a general ledger. It labels tax as GST, takes an ABN, formats a BSB and bills in Australian dollars. If you actually need bank feeds, payroll and BAS in one place, the right answer is Xero or MYOB instead.

Is there a free QuickBooks alternative in Australia?

Yes, three of them. Invoice-Generator.com.au is free with no signup, no watermark and no invoice limit; Zoho Invoice is free at the time of writing; and Wave is free in Australia at the time of writing, though its GST handling is generic rather than ATO-shaped. Thriday is free under A$50,000 of revenue at the time of writing if you bank with them.

Who should stay on QuickBooks rather than switch?

Stay if you run payroll through it, track inventory in it, have years of reconciled bank-feed history you would have to rebuild, or have an accountant who works in it and charges you for the privilege of not. QuickBooks is capable accounting software with real Australian GST and BAS support. Switching costs you migration time, and simpler is only cheaper if you were not using the accounting in the first place.

Do I need accounting software, or just an invoicing app?

If you send invoices, get paid by bank transfer and hand a shoebox to an accountant once a year, you need an invoicing app. If you have staff on payroll, stock on shelves, or a quarterly BAS you lodge yourself from your own books, you need accounting software. Buying the second when you needed the first is the most common reason people go looking for a QuickBooks alternative.

Which QuickBooks alternatives are built in Australia?

Rounded and Thriday are both Australian-built and priced in Australian dollars, and MYOB is the long-standing Australian accounting incumbent. Australian-built matters less than Australian-shaped: what you are checking is GST rather than sales tax, an ABN field rather than an EIN field, a BSB rather than a routing number, and a price that does not move with the exchange rate.
