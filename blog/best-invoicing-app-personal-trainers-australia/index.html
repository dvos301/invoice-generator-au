How we tested: the session-pack test. Three questions per tool. Can it invoice a 10-session pack up front as a single line, ideally with a deposit option? Can it then bill a recurring monthly membership automatically, without you opening the app? And can it tell you who has sessions left to run?

That test matters because a personal trainer bills in two shapes that pull in opposite directions. A pack is one big invoice, paid before any work happens. A membership is a small invoice, repeated forever. Most trainers run both at the same time, plus the occasional one-off session and, if they contract to a gym, an invoice to the gym as well. An app that does the first shape and not the second leaves you retyping the second one 12 times a year per member.

The worked number. Take 14 clients on an $850 ten-session pack. That is 14 × $850 = $11,900 invoiced up front and 14 × 10 = 140 sessions still to deliver. At one session a week each, the money lands in a single week and the work runs for the next ten. Your bank balance says you had a $11,900 month; your diary says you have $11,900 of unpaid labour ahead of you. That gap is the thing PT billing gets wrong, and no invoicing app will warn you about it.

It also creates a real exposure. At $85 a session, a client who stops after five sessions leaves five undelivered sessions, or $425, sitting in your account that arguably is not yours yet. If three of those 14 clients drop at the halfway mark, that is 3 × 5 × $85 = $1,275 you have banked against work you will never do. Decide your refund and expiry policy before you send the first pack invoice, write it on the invoice, and keep the money visible. Our guide to invoice payment terms in Australia covers where that wording goes.

Who fails the test. All eight pass question one: a pack is just a quantity-10 line item, and InvoiceSonic can additionally take a percentage or fixed deposit on it. Only two pass question two with a published feature: InvoiceSonic on Pro, and Bookipi on every plan including free. And all eight fail question three. Not one of them tracks sessions delivered against sessions sold, because invoicing apps track invoices, not attendance. That is the single most useful thing to know before you spend a weekend comparing feature tables, and it is why the last section of this post sends some readers to a different kind of software entirely.

1. InvoiceSonic — Best overall

InvoiceSonic is an invoicing app that builds a professional tax invoice in about a minute, takes a deposit on it if you want one, and on Pro sends a recurring invoice on a schedule and chases it if nobody pays. For a trainer running packs and memberships side by side, that combination is the whole job.

It passes the first two questions of the session-pack test cleanly. A pack goes out as one line with a quantity, and you can ask for a percentage or fixed deposit up front, which is how you split an $850 pack into $425 now and $425 at session five without inventing a workaround — further stages are raised as separate invoices. Memberships run on recurring invoices with automatic reminders, invoice open tracking tells you the moment a client has looked at it, and an email receipt goes out automatically when you mark it paid, which kills the Sunday-night "did that go through?" text. Online quote acceptance turns a 12-week transformation proposal into an invoice in one click. Clients pay by bank transfer or PayID with no gateway to set up and no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so an $850 pack arrives as $850. Tax handling is localised across supported markets, which in practice means the ABN field, the BSB format and the 10% GST label are already correct. There is an iPhone app; there is no native Android app, so Android users run the web app and add it to the home screen. On question three it fails like everything else: an invoice tracker tells you about invoices, not about sessions.

Pros

  • Recurring invoices and automatic reminders on Pro, so a membership run bills itself every month.
  • Percentage or fixed deposits, which is the clean way to bill half a pack up front.
  • Bank transfer and PayID with no InvoiceSonic transaction fee — nothing shaved off an $850 pack.

Cons

  • Recurring billing is Pro only. A free account is capped at 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month, which 14 clients blow through in a week.
  • No session tracking, no scheduling and no timesheets, so it will never tell you that a client has four sessions left.

Zoho Invoice is free — Zoho states no cost and no contracts — and unlike a PDF generator it sends the invoice, saves the client and keeps the history. For a trainer with 14 regulars and no budget, that saved client list is the difference between a filing system and a shoebox.

On the session-pack test it invoices a pack properly and formats an Australian invoice correctly, but an automatic recurring send is not something we could confirm on the Australian page at the time of writing, so check it before you build a membership run on it. The trade-off is the surroundings. Zoho Invoice sits inside a very large product suite, the interface is dense next to a single-purpose invoicing app, and real support is a paid-suite affair. Multi-language invoicing across 19-plus languages is a genuine advantage if you train clients who would rather read an invoice in their first language.

Pros

Cons

For a part-time trainer with two or three ongoing clients and a day job, recurring billing at nothing is genuinely good value.

What to look for in an invoicing app for personal trainers

PT billing is unusual because the money and the work happen at different times. A pack is paid before delivery, a membership repeats forever, and a gym contract invoice waits on someone else's payment run. These six things decide which app survives that.

  • Recurring billing, not a saved template. A saved template still needs you to open the app, change the date and press send. Fourteen members is 168 invoices a year at 12 a client — a recurring invoice goes out whether you remember or not, and only two apps here publish the feature.
  • A pack that invoices as one line, with a deposit option. Ten sessions should be a quantity-10 line at $85, not ten separate invoices. If a client wants to split it, a percentage deposit is cleaner than an IOU written on the back of a program card.
  • Automatic reminders for the invoices that are not paid up front. Packs get paid immediately; gym contracts and corporate sessions do not. Reminders on the due date and again a week later recover most of them without a phone call, and your payment terms are what the reminder is enforcing.
  • A proper Australian tax invoice with your ABN on it. Where you are registered for GST the invoice must carry the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied including quantity and price, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. If you contract to a gym without an ABN the payer may be required to withhold 47% of the payment, which is a brutal way to learn the rule — see whether you need an ABN to invoice.
  • The payment fee, not just the subscription. On $59,500 of pack billings, card payments at 2.2% cost $1,309.00 a year while PayID and bank transfer cost nothing. That single line is worth more than every feature comparison on this page, because it is nearly eight times the annual price of the app itself.
  • Whether your problem is invoicing or session tracking. If the question keeping you up is "how much has Sarah paid me", an invoicing app fixes it. If it is "how many sessions does Sarah have left", none of these eight will, ever.

Which one should you choose?

  • If you train three or four clients and invoice a pack now and then → the free Australian tax invoice generator, and email the PDF.
  • If you run session packs and monthly memberships for 10 to 25 clients → InvoiceSonic on Pro, so the memberships bill and chase themselves.
  • If you want sending and a saved client list for nothing → Zoho Invoice, or Wave if you also want basic books and can check the GST wording yourself.
  • If your clients only ever pay by card → Square Invoices, with the 2.2% written into your pack price rather than absorbed.
  • If the tax bill on $11,900 of pack money is what frightens you → Rounded, for invoicing, expenses and a tax estimate in one Australian app.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. If what you actually need is session balances, class rosters, bookings and door check-ins, you are looking at the wrong category of software. Gym and studio booking-and-membership platforms such as Mindbody, Hapana and Glofox are built around the timetable and the membership, and billing is a module inside them — they are not invoicing apps, which is why we have not ranked them here, and they are priced for a studio rather than a sole trader. InvoiceSonic does no scheduling, timesheets or session tracking at all, and it is not accounting software: no bank feeds, no BAS lodgement, no payroll. If you want your tax and expenses handled alongside the invoicing, Rounded is Australian-built for sole traders and Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing; InvoiceSonic Pro is US$14.99/month on monthly billing. If you employ trainers, run payroll and lodge a quarterly BAS, that is accounting territory: Xero or MYOB. And a free PDF generator, ours included, is the wrong choice the moment the same membership invoice has to go out every month.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best invoicing app for personal trainers in Australia?

InvoiceSonic. It invoices a session pack up front, can take a percentage or fixed deposit on it, and on Pro it bills a recurring monthly membership and chases it with automatic reminders. Bookipi is the only other app here that lists recurring invoices on its free plan, and that plan stops at 3 invoices a month. If you only need one compliant PDF, Invoice-Generator.com.au builds an ATO-compliant tax invoice with no signup.

Can an invoicing app bill a 10-session pack and a monthly membership?

Yes. Every app in this list can invoice a pack up front as a quantity-10 line. The recurring monthly membership is the part that separates them: only InvoiceSonic on Pro and Bookipi on every plan publish recurring invoices as a confirmed feature. Square Invoices, Zoho Invoice, Rounded, Wave and Xero did not state an automatic recurring send on the pages we checked at the time of writing.

Do personal trainers have to charge GST in Australia?

Only once you are registered for GST, which is compulsory at A$75,000 of turnover. Fourteen clients buying five $850 ten-packs a year is $59,500, which sits under the threshold, so plenty of solo trainers are not registered and do not charge 10% GST. The 89th pack in a year takes you to $75,650 and over the line, and any income you earn contracting to a gym counts towards the same figure.

Is there a genuinely free invoicing app for personal trainers?

Yes. Invoice-Generator.com.au is free with no signup, no watermark and no invoice limit, Zoho Invoice is free at the time of writing, and Wave is free in Australia at the time of writing. InvoiceSonic lets you create one invoice with no sign-up and no credit card, then a free account gives you 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month.

Can an invoicing app track how many sessions a client has left?

None of the eight apps in this list does. Invoicing apps track invoices and payments, not sessions delivered, so InvoiceSonic will tell you a pack invoice was opened and paid but not that the client has four sessions to run. Session balances, class rosters and check-ins are what gym and studio booking-and-membership platforms such as Mindbody, Hapana and Glofox are built for, and they are a different category of software. Until you need one, put the quantity on the invoice line as 10 x 60-minute sessions and keep the tally yourself.
