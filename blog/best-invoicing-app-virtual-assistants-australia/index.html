How we tested: the retainer-plus-overage test. Three questions per tool. Can it send a fixed monthly retainer on a schedule, without you opening the app? Can it count the hours that ran past the retainer, tracked rather than guessed? And can both land on one invoice, carrying an ABN and the right GST treatment?

The third question is the one that costs money. A 20-hour retainer at $65 an hour is $1,300 a month, and six overage hours is another $390. Split those across two invoices, or leave the extra to an email at month end, and you have put 30% of the month's revenue into a conversation the client did not budget for. Over a year that overage is 72 hours and $4,680, on top of $15,600 of retainer.

Who fails the test. Every tool fails at least one question, and none of the eight publishes a confirmed pass on all three. Only InvoiceSonic states recurring invoicing, on Pro; only Harvest and FreshBooks state time tracking; no tool says a scheduled invoice will pick up tracked hours before it sends. Every workable setup is one app for the retainer, one place to count hours, and a minute of typing a month.

1. InvoiceSonic — Best overall

InvoiceSonic automates the half of VA billing that never changes. On Pro, recurring invoices and automatic reminders are both included, so a $1,300 retainer goes out on the first and chases itself if the client is slow.

It passes question one and fails question two: there is no timesheet in it, so six overage hours are counted elsewhere and typed in as a line. Online quote acceptance turns a scoping call into an invoice in one click, and open tracking shows when a client reads it. For overseas work, localised tax labels, tax ID formats and currency symbols across 19 countries mean a UK client sees a familiar document. Australian clients pay by bank transfer or PayID with no InvoiceSonic transaction fee.

Pros

  • Recurring invoices and automatic reminders on Pro, so the fixed part of a retainer bills itself twelve times a year.
  • Localised tax labels, tax ID formats and currency symbols across 19 countries, which matters when clients are overseas.

Cons

  • No timesheets or time tracking, so overage hours are counted in another tool and typed in by hand.
  • The free plan stops at 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month, the 7-day Pro trial asks for a card, and there is no native Android app.

Price: at the time of writing, the free plan is A$0 and the first invoice needs no sign-up or card; Pro is A$19 a month, or A$168 a year billed annually, which is A$14 a month, saves A$60 and is price-locked while the subscription runs. Best for: VAs on monthly retainers who want the fixed fee billed and chased untouched.

3. Harvest — Best for tracking the hours that run over

Harvest is a timer first and an invoicing tool second, which makes it the strongest answer here to the second half of the test. If your arguments with clients are about hours, this app produces the evidence.

Track time against a client and the overage is a report, not a memory. Harvest does not publish an automatic recurring retainer invoice, so question one is unconfirmed, and GST handling is generic. The free plan is the catch: one seat and two projects, so it runs out on your third client. Team pricing is in USD: a natural hedge if you bill American clients in USD, exchange-rate risk if you do not.

Pros

  • Time tracking is the core product, so overage hours are recorded as you work, not reconstructed at month end.
  • A genuinely free tier for one user, enough for a client or two.

Cons

  • Two projects on the free plan is very few for a VA, and no recurring retainer invoice is listed.
  • Priced in USD and weak on GST, so an Australian tax invoice takes manual setup.

Price: at the time of writing, free for 1 seat and 2 projects; the Teams plan starts from US$9 per seat per month on annual billing. Best for: VAs billing mostly by tracked hours.

4. FreshBooks — Best for hours, expenses and invoices in one app

FreshBooks puts invoicing, time tracking and expenses in one place, which covers more of a VA's month than a pure invoicing tool. If you rebill software or ads, the expense sits next to the timer.

It half-passes. Time tracking is stated; an automatic recurring retainer is not, so check question one on a trial. Plans are in USD and the lower tiers carry client limits, an odd constraint for a business built on a few long clients. GST is generic rather than ATO-shaped. Lite at US$23 a month is roughly the order of InvoiceSonic Pro billed monthly at A$19 at the time of writing, but only one of the two publishes recurring invoicing.

Pros

Cons

  • USD pricing and client limits on the lower tiers, with no free plan to test a month on.
  • GST is generic rather than ATO-shaped, and recurring retainer billing is not listed.

Price: at the time of writing, Lite US$23, Plus US$43 and Premium US$70 per month. Best for: VAs with several clients and billable expenses who want one app.

If you track hours in a separate timer, that is a real reason to pick it.

7. Bonsai — Best for the contract before the retainer

Bonsai is unconfirmed on questions one and two: neither recurring billing nor time tracking is stated in what we could verify. It is also priced and shaped for United States freelancers, from USD $24 to $89 a month at the time of writing, with no AU GST specialisation.

Pros

Price: at the time of writing, USD $24 to $89 per month across four plans. Best for: VAs who sell scoped packages and want the contract and the invoice together.

What to look for in an invoicing app for virtual assistants

VA billing has a shape most invoicing software was not designed for: one fixed amount that repeats forever, plus a variable amount nobody agreed in advance. Judge tools on that shape, not on feature counts.

  • Recurring invoicing, not a saved template. A saved template still needs you to open the app, change the date and press send twelve times a year per client.
  • A place the overage hours are counted. In the invoicing app or a separate timer, the number has to come from a record. Six hours reconstructed from memory is six hours you will discount when challenged.
  • Written overage terms, on the invoice. State the included hours, the rate past them and when the extra is billed; our guide to invoice payment terms in Australia covers the wording and a fair due date.
  • Multi-currency handling you understand. Plenty of Australian VAs bill in USD or GBP. Agree the currency in writing and reconcile the AUD that actually landed, not the figure on the invoice, because the rate moves between sending and settlement. InvoiceSonic carries tax labels and currency symbols for 19 countries; the conversion half is your bank's.
  • Correct GST treatment on overseas work. Exports of services to a client outside Australia are often GST-free, but the rules carry exceptions and turn on where the service is used. Check your circumstances with the ATO or your accountant before switching GST off, and read the GST registration threshold if you are near A$75,000.
  • Invoice numbering you will not regret. Retainers plus ad-hoc jobs across several clients get messy fast; how to number invoices sets up a sequence you can explain to an auditor.

Which one should you choose?

Run the numbers on four retainer clients: $62,400 a year of retainer at our worked rate, plus the overage. At the time of writing that costs A$0 with the free generator or Zoho Invoice, A$168 a year with InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually, $99 a year with Rounded Solo, from US$9 per seat per month with Harvest Teams, US$23 a month with FreshBooks Lite, from USD $24 a month with Bonsai, and $446.16 a year in card fees with Square on one client's billings.

  • If you are one or two clients in and testing the water → the free Australian tax invoice generator, emailed as a PDF.
  • If you run three or more monthly retainers → InvoiceSonic on Pro, with the overage added as a line each month.
  • If most of your income is tracked hours rather than a fixed fee → Harvest, and accept the two-project free cap.
  • If you want an Australian app that also estimates your tax → Rounded Solo.
  • If a client will only pay by card → Square Invoices, with the 2.2% written into your rate.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. If the bulk of your billing is hours rather than a retainer, InvoiceSonic is the wrong shape: it has no timesheets, and Harvest or FreshBooks record the work as you do it. If you want a tax estimate and expenses alongside the invoicing, Rounded is Australian-built for sole traders and Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing, less than InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually. If your problem is the contract rather than the invoice, Bonsai puts proposals and terms in the same place. And a free PDF generator, ours included, is the wrong choice the moment the same invoice goes out every month: it builds the document, it never sends one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best invoicing app for virtual assistants in Australia?

InvoiceSonic. On Pro a fixed monthly retainer goes out on a schedule and chases itself when a client is late, which covers the half of VA billing that never changes. No app here publishes a confirmed way to fold this month's overage hours into that scheduled invoice, so add them yourself as a second line.

Can an invoicing app bill a monthly retainer and the overage hours on one invoice?

Not automatically, on the evidence we could verify. InvoiceSonic Pro publishes recurring invoices, and Harvest and FreshBooks publish time tracking, but no tool here states that a scheduled invoice will collect tracked hours before it sends. Track the hours in one place, then add one line each month.

Do Australian virtual assistants charge GST on overseas clients?

Only if you are registered for GST, which is compulsory at A$75,000 of turnover. Exports of services to a client outside Australia are often GST-free, but the rules carry exceptions and turn on where the work is used, so confirm your own situation with the ATO or your accountant before switching GST off.

How should a VA invoice a client who pays in US dollars?

Bill in the currency you agreed in writing, then record what actually landed in your Australian account after conversion, because that AUD figure is what your bookkeeping and GST turnover run on.
