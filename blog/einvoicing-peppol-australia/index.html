eInvoicing & Peppol in Australia: A Plain-English Guide

IG
Dyllan Vosloo, Founder 16 July 2026 · 6 min read

eInvoicing is the direct exchange of invoices between the sender's and receiver's accounting software as structured data — not as a PDF or a piece of paper. In Australia it runs on the Peppol network, and the ATO is the official Peppol Authority. For most private businesses it's voluntary, but it can mean faster payment and far less manual data entry. Here's how it works and how to get started.

What is eInvoicing?

With traditional invoicing, you create an invoice, save it as a PDF or print it, then email or post it. The person receiving it has to read it and re-key the details into their own system — slow, and easy to get wrong.

eInvoicing removes that step. The invoice is sent as structured digital data straight from your accounting software into your customer's accounting software. There's no PDF to open and no typing at the other end: the invoice simply appears in the buyer's system, ready to be checked and paid. Because the data is standardised, both systems understand it automatically.

What is Peppol?

For eInvoicing to work, every business's software needs to "speak" the same language. That's what Peppol provides. Peppol is an international framework — a set of agreed standards and a secure network — that lets businesses on completely different software exchange invoices reliably. It's used across Europe, New Zealand and elsewhere, and Australia has adopted it too.

In Australia, the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) is the Peppol Authority, which means it oversees how the framework operates locally. You don't connect to the ATO to send an eInvoice — you connect through the Peppol network via your software or an access-point provider — but the ATO governs the standards.

How eInvoicing works

The mechanics are straightforward once you're set up:

  1. You raise an invoice in your accounting or invoicing software as usual.
  2. Your software sends it over the Peppol network through an access point (your software provider or a service provider acts as this access point).
  3. The buyer's access point receives it and delivers the structured data into their accounting software.
  4. The invoice appears in the buyer's system, ready to review and pay — no re-keying.

Businesses are identified on the network using their ABN, which is how the system knows where to route each invoice.

The benefits of eInvoicing

  • Faster payment. Because invoices land directly in the buyer's system, they can be processed sooner. The Australian Government has committed to paying Peppol eInvoices under eligible contracts faster than standard terms, which helps supplier cash flow.
  • Fewer errors. No manual re-keying means fewer typos, wrong amounts and misdirected invoices.
  • Lower cost. Less paper, less printing and less admin time per invoice.
  • Better security. Invoices travel through a secure, registered network rather than email, reducing the risk of invoice fraud and fake billing scams.
  • Less lost paperwork. Invoices can't get stuck in a spam folder or sit unopened in an inbox.

Is eInvoicing mandatory?

This is where it's important to be precise. For most private businesses, eInvoicing is voluntary — there is no general law forcing private companies to adopt it. You can choose to use it because of the benefits above, not because you're required to.

The one firm requirement sits with government: all Australian Commonwealth government agencies are required to be able to receive Peppol eInvoices. So if you supply goods or services to a Commonwealth agency, you have the option to send them eInvoices and they must be able to accept them. That is a requirement on the agencies to be able to receive — not a mandate forcing private businesses to send. Some state governments also encourage or support eInvoicing, but you should check the current position for your own situation rather than assume a blanket obligation.

How to get started

Adopting eInvoicing is usually simpler than people expect:

  1. Check your software. Many popular accounting and invoicing platforms already support Peppol eInvoicing — you may only need to switch it on.
  2. Or use a service provider. If your software doesn't support it directly, an eInvoicing (access point) service provider can connect you to the Peppol network.
  3. Register on the network using your ABN so other businesses can find and send to you.
  4. Tell your trading partners you can send and receive eInvoices, and ask whether they can too.

For the current, authoritative steps and a list of supporting software, see the government's guidance at business.gov.au/finance/payments-and-invoicing/einvoicing and the ATO eInvoicing pages.

Frequently asked questions

What is eInvoicing?

eInvoicing is the direct, digital exchange of invoices between a supplier's and a buyer's accounting software. The invoice travels as structured data over a secure network rather than as a PDF or paper document, so it lands straight in the buyer's system with no manual data entry.

What is Peppol?

Peppol is the international framework Australia uses for eInvoicing. It sets common standards so businesses on different software can exchange invoices reliably. The ATO is the Australian Peppol Authority, overseeing the framework locally.

Is eInvoicing mandatory in Australia?

eInvoicing is voluntary for most private businesses. All Australian Commonwealth government agencies are required to be able to receive Peppol eInvoices, so if you supply the Commonwealth you can send them eInvoices — but there is no general mandate forcing private businesses to adopt it.

How do I get started with eInvoicing?

Check whether your accounting or invoicing software already supports Peppol eInvoicing — many providers do — and enable it, or connect through an eInvoicing service provider. Once registered on the Peppol network, you can send and receive eInvoices with other registered businesses.
