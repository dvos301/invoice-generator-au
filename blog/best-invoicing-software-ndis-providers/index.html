How we tested: the plan-manager test. Three questions per tool. Can the invoice carry the participant's name and NDIS number as their own labelled details rather than buried in a description? Can each line hold the support item number from the NDIS Price Guide or Support Catalogue alongside the service dates and hours delivered? And can you issue it GST-free, with your ABN showing and no 10% line added out of habit?

Three billing routes, three different jobs. Plan-managed participants are the reason this test exists: you send the invoice to a registered plan manager, a third party who checks it against the plan before paying, so a formatting fault is caught by someone who does not know you and does not ring to ask. Self-managed participants are billed directly and claim the money back from the NDIA themselves, which usually means a plainer invoice and a faster answer. NDIA-managed supports are claimed through the myplace provider portal rather than emailed as an invoice, so your software is your own record of the work rather than the claim channel. Most solo support workers end up doing all three, from the same template.

What a rejection actually costs. Say you bill A$4,000 of supports a month. That is about $133 a day of income. A rejected invoice is not a five-minute fix: it goes back for correction and rejoins the next processing run, which on the reports we see costs 14 to 21 days. Call it 15. Now note that a formatting fault is almost never one invoice — a missing NDIS number, the wrong support item number or a 10% GST line on a GST-free support comes off the template, so every invoice you send that month carries it. Fifteen days at $133 a day is roughly A$2,000 permanently out of your account: you take the hit once, and the hole stays open for as long as you keep billing at the later cadence. Fixing the template is worth $2,000 of working capital before it is worth a minute of your time.

Who fails the test. Only InvoiceSonic passes all three questions with a purpose-built layout. The free generator on this site fails question one and two on labelling — you type the NDIS number and support item numbers into the description yourself — and fails outright on sending, because it makes a PDF and nothing else. Zoho Invoice, Square Invoices, Xero, Bookipi, Rounded and Wave all send properly formatted Australian invoices, but every one of them makes you build the NDIS layout out of generic invoice fields, and none of them stated an NDIS template at the time of writing. Wave is the weakest on question three: its GST handling is generic rather than ATO-shaped, which is a poor place to start when the whole point is a GST-free supply with an ABN on it.

What to look for in invoicing software for NDIS providers

NDIS billing is checked billing. Someone who has never met you reads the invoice against a plan before any money moves, so the layout is worth more than the feature list. These are the six things that decide whether an invoice clears first time.

  • A place for the participant's name and NDIS number. These belong in labelled details, not appended to a line description where a checker has to hunt for them. This is the single most common reason a first invoice comes back.
  • Support item numbers, service dates and hours on every line. Each line should show the support item number from the NDIS Price Guide or Support Catalogue, the date the support was delivered and the hours. Never guess a support item number or a price limit — take both from the participant's plan and the current Support Catalogue.
  • GST-free handling. Most NDIS supports are GST-free where they are a reasonable and necessary support in a participant's plan, so your invoice will often show no GST even though you are registered for GST. Adding 10% out of habit is a rejection. Confirm your own circumstances with the ATO or your accountant — this is a tax question about your business, not a setting in an app.
  • Your ABN on every invoice. Without an ABN the payer may be required to withhold 47% of the payment, and the wider tax invoice requirements still apply to the parts of your work that are taxable.
  • Automatic reminders. Plan managers pay from a queue and things get missed. A reminder on the due date and another a week later recovers most of it without a phone call; our guide on how to chase overdue invoices in Australia covers what to do when that fails.
  • Which management type you are billing. Plan-managed goes to the plan manager, self-managed goes to the participant, and NDIA-managed is claimed in the myplace provider portal. Software that stores participants separately saves you sending the wrong invoice to the wrong place.

None of the eight tools on this page is an NDIS practice-management system, and none of them is NDIS-approved or NDIS-registered — that status applies to providers, not software. Once you are rostering support workers, running SIL houses, tracking shift-based pay or lodging bulk claim files, an invoicing app is the wrong tool and dedicated NDIS software is what you need: InvoiceSonic does no rostering, timesheets or staff management, and a free PDF generator does not even send. If your books are the problem rather than your invoices — mixed GST-free and taxable income, a quarterly BAS, a bookkeeper — that is accounting software territory, so Xero or MYOB. And if you want tax and expenses handled alongside the invoicing, Rounded is Australian-built for sole traders and Solo is cheaper per year than InvoiceSonic Pro billed annually at the time of writing. Nothing on this page is tax advice: check your GST position with the ATO or your accountant.

What has to be on an NDIS invoice?

An NDIS invoice needs the participant's name and NDIS number, the support item number from the NDIS Price Guide or Support Catalogue, the service dates, the hours delivered, and your ABN. If you are registered for GST the ATO also sets out what a tax invoice must carry: the words Tax invoice, your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more.

Do you charge GST on NDIS invoices?

Usually not. Most NDIS supports are GST-free where they are a reasonable and necessary support in a participant's plan, so an NDIS invoice often shows no GST even when the provider is registered for GST. That is why adding a 10% line by habit is one of the most common reasons an invoice comes back. Confirm your own circumstances with the ATO or your accountant rather than relying on a template.

Is there free invoicing software for NDIS providers?

Yes. Invoice-Generator.com.au is free with no signup, no watermark and no invoice limit, and Zoho Invoice and Wave were both free at the time of writing. A free InvoiceSonic account gives you 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month, and the very first invoice needs no sign-up and no credit card.

How do you invoice plan-managed, self-managed and NDIA-managed participants?

Plan-managed participants are billed by sending the invoice to their registered plan manager, who checks it and pays you. Self-managed participants are billed directly, and they claim the money back from the NDIA themselves. NDIA-managed supports are claimed through the myplace provider portal rather than by emailing an invoice, so your invoicing software is your own record of the work, not the claim channel.
