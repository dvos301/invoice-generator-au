How we tested: the gap-fee test. Three questions per tool. Can it invoice the parent's remaining gap fee on a recurring weekly or fortnightly cycle, after subsidy is applied, without you rebuilding the invoice? Can you change one line — a day added, a day missed — without starting again? And does it produce a proper Australian tax invoice with your ABN on it?

A worked illustration — and it is only an illustration. Take one family with a place three days a week at $130 a day. The full fee is $390 a week. If that family's subsidy covered 70%, the gap fee you invoice the parent would be $117 a week — 52 recurring invoices a year for one family. Treat the 70% as a made-up number for the arithmetic. What a family actually receives depends on their own income and activity level, so ask them to check their own rate with Services Australia rather than invoicing on an assumption.

1. InvoiceSonic — Best overall

InvoiceSonic builds a professional tax invoice in about a minute, and on Pro it sends that invoice on a schedule and chases it if nobody pays. For a weekly gap fee that is the whole job: set the family up once a term, then leave it.

It passes all three questions. Invoice open tracking shows the moment a parent has looked at the invoice, and an email receipt goes out when you mark it paid. Families pay by bank transfer or PayID with no gateway to set up and no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so a $117 gap fee arrives as $117. Australian tax handling is built in — ABN field, BSB formatting, 10% GST label — as part of localised support across supported markets. There is an iPhone app and no native Android app.

Pros

  • Recurring invoices and automatic reminders on Pro, so 52 weekly gap fees per family go out untouched.
  • Bank transfer and PayID with no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so nothing is shaved off a small weekly amount.
  • Pro offers monthly and annual billing; check the live USD price before subscribing.

Cons

  • No attendance records, timesheets or staff management, so it sits alongside your attendance system.
  • Recurring billing is Pro only. A free account is capped at 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month, which one weekly family uses up inside the first month.

It half-passes. It sends, and it formats an Australian invoice properly, but an automatic recurring send was not something we could confirm. The trade-off is the surroundings: a very large product suite, a dense interface, and support aimed at paying customers.

Pros

Cons

  • Support is aimed at paid-suite customers, and recurring invoicing is not listed.

Price: free at the time of writing. Best for: educators who want sending and a saved family list for A$0 and can live with a busy interface.

4. Square Invoices — Best if parents want to pay by card

Square Invoices gives you unlimited free invoices, estimates and automatic reminders, and the parent pays by tapping a card link in the email. When the gap fee lands in an awkward week, a card is sometimes the difference between paid and chased.

It half-passes: automatic reminders are on the free plan, but a recurring send is not stated, and estimate auto-convert and milestone schedules are Plus only. The real cost is the card fee. At 2.2% on online card payments, a $117 weekly gap fee is $6,084 a year per family, so the fee is $133.85 a year for one family and $535.39 a year across four. The same families paying by PayID cost you nothing.

Pros

  • Unlimited free invoices with automatic reminders on the free plan.
  • Parents pay by card from the invoice itself, which often gets it paid the same day.

Cons

  • Milestone schedules and estimate auto-convert sit behind Plus, and a recurring send is not listed.

Price: at the time of writing, free, or Plus at AUD $30 per month; 2.2% on online card payments. Best for: educators whose families will only pay by card.

What to look for in invoicing software for childcare providers

Childcare billing is the most repetitive money job in the care sector: small amounts, the same families, every week, and the number moves whenever a child is away. Editing and re-sending beats any feature list, and the payment fee usually costs more than the subscription.

  • Recurring invoicing you can edit before it sends. A saved template still needs you to open it, change the dates and press send 52 times a family. A recurring invoice you can adjust for one missed day is the requirement.
  • A clear breakdown. Show the sessions billed, the full fee, the subsidy applied and the gap fee left to pay. A parent who can see all four lines does not email you about the fifth.
  • A proper Australian tax invoice. Where you are registered for GST it must carry the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. The full list of tax invoice requirements is also on the ATO site.
  • Payment terms that suit a weekly amount. Seven days on a $117 invoice behaves nothing like 30 days on a $5,000 one; our guide to invoice payment terms sets out the wording.
  • Automatic reminders. A reminder on the due date and another a week later recovers most late payments without an awkward conversation at the door, which matters when you see the payer every morning. Chasing overdue invoices covers the rest.
  • The payment fee, priced on your real volume. Bank transfer and PayID cost nothing; card at 2.2% costs $535.39 a year on $24,336 of gap fees. The percentage is the whole decision.

Which one should you choose?

  • If you have one or two families and invoice occasionally → the free Australian tax invoice generator, and email the PDF.
  • If you bill three or more families every week → InvoiceSonic on Pro, so the gap fees invoice and chase themselves.
  • If you want sending and a saved family list for nothing → Zoho Invoice, and accept the dense interface.
  • If you also want your tax estimated as you go → Rounded, at $99 a year for Solo regular AUD at the time of writing.
  • If families will only pay by card → Square Invoices, with the 2.2% built into your fee rather than absorbed.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. If your job includes recording attendance and claiming subsidy, an invoicing app is the wrong category of software. Childcare management systems such as Xplor, QikKids and Kidsoft exist for attendance, session reporting and Child Care Subsidy claiming, and nothing on this page does any of that. Which system you need, and what your obligations are as a provider, is a question for your own advisers, not for us. If you want tax and expenses handled alongside the invoicing, Rounded is Australian-built for sole traders and Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing; InvoiceSonic Pro is US$14.99/month on monthly billing. With staff, a bookkeeper and a quarterly BAS, that is accounting software territory — Xero or MYOB. A free generator, ours included, is the wrong choice the moment the same invoice has to go out more than once.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best invoicing software for childcare providers in Australia?

InvoiceSonic. Recurring invoices and automatic reminders are both on Pro, so a weekly gap fee goes out on a schedule and chases itself if a family is late. Bookipi is the only other tool here that lists recurring invoices on a free plan, and that plan stops at 3 invoices a month.

How do you invoice a family for the gap fee?

You invoice the parent for the part of your fee that subsidy does not cover. As an illustration only: three days a week at $130 a day is a full fee of $390 a week, and if a family's subsidy covered 70% the gap fee would be $117 a week. How much subsidy a family receives depends on their income and activity level, so ask them to check their own entitlement with Services Australia.

Can invoicing software handle a week where a child was absent?

Only if you can edit one line on the scheduled invoice instead of rebuilding it. A recurring invoice you can open, adjust and send is the difference between a two-minute correction and a fifteen-minute rebuild. Nothing in this list tracks attendance for you.

Is there genuinely free invoicing software for childcare providers?

Yes. Invoice-Generator.com.au is free with no signup, no watermark and no invoice limit, and Zoho Invoice and Wave are both free at the time of writing. A free InvoiceSonic account gives you 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month.

What has to be on a childcare invoice in Australia?

If you are registered for GST the ATO requires the words Tax invoice, your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. Parents also expect the child's name, the dates billed, the full fee, the subsidy applied and the gap fee left to pay.
