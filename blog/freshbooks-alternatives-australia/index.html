How we tested: the AUD-and-client-cap test. Two things push Australians off FreshBooks, and neither is a missing feature. The first is USD pricing, which means your invoicing cost moves with the exchange rate and your card statement never matches your budget. The second is the billable-client limit on the cheaper tiers, which turns a growing client list into a forced upgrade. So we asked every tool two questions: does it publish a price in Australian dollars, and does it cap how many clients you may invoice?

First, the case for staying on FreshBooks. FreshBooks is genuinely good at what it does. Invoicing, time tracking and expenses sit together properly, which is the right shape for a service business that bills hours and recharges costs. Nothing in this list matches that combination in one app at one price. The honest push factors for Australians are narrower than "FreshBooks is bad": Lite, Plus and Premium are US$23, US$43 and US$70 a month at the time of writing, so you carry currency risk on a monthly bill; the cheaper tiers limit billable clients, so growth costs money before it earns any; and GST handling is generic rather than ATO-shaped, so your tax invoice requirements are met by configuration rather than by default. If none of those three is hurting you, do not migrate. Switching invoicing software mid-year is a day of work and a fortnight of small errors.

1. InvoiceSonic — Best overall

For someone leaving FreshBooks, the features that matter are the ones that used to be the reason you paid: recurring invoices and automatic reminders, invoice open tracking so you can see the moment a client reads it, online quote acceptance that turns an accepted quote into an invoice in one click, and an email receipt the moment you mark an invoice paid. Australian tax handling is built in rather than configured — the ABN field, the BSB format and the 10% GST label are part of localised support across supported markets. Clients pay by bank transfer or PayID with no gateway to set up and no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so an A$2,400 invoice arrives as A$2,400. Subscription payments run through Stripe and full card details are never stored.

Pros

  • Invoices can be issued in AUD; Pro subscriptions are priced in USD.
  • Unlimited professional invoices on Pro, so adding clients never triggers a tier upgrade.
  • Bank transfer and PayID with no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, so nothing is shaved off the invoice.

Cons

  • No time tracking, timesheets or expense tracking, which is exactly what FreshBooks users tend to be paying for. If you bill hours logged in the app, this is a downgrade.
  • The free plan is capped at 3 saved invoices, 1 tracked email send and 1 automatic reminder a month, and the 7-day Pro trial requires a card up front.

Invoice-Generator.com.au is a free tax invoice generator that produces a finished PDF in your browser with no signup, no watermark, no ads and no invoice limit. On the test it passes both halves in the most literal way possible: the price is A$0 and there is no client list to cap.

Price: free, always. Best for: anyone cancelling a subscription who sends a handful of invoices a month and does not need sending or tracking.

3. Rounded — Best Australian-built option

Rounded is built in Australia specifically for sole traders and freelancers, and it does invoicing, expenses and tax estimates in one place. That makes it the closest thing here to FreshBooks in shape, priced in Australian dollars instead of US dollars.

It passes the currency half cleanly and no billable-client limit is listed. Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing; InvoiceSonic Pro is US$14.99/month on monthly billing. Rounded tracks expenses and estimates your tax; compare its annual price with FreshBooks Lite’s current price before switching. That is a real reason to pick it over our number one. The catch is that there is no free plan at all, so you cannot run a quiet month on it to see whether you like it, and Pro at $70 a month regular AUD is a big step up from Solo if you outgrow the cheaper tier. Promotional pricing varies, so check what year two costs before you commit.

Pros

  • Australian-built for sole traders, so expenses and tax estimates are shaped for the ATO rather than adapted.
  • Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing — the cheapest paid subscription in this list.

Cons

  • No free plan, so there is no way to trial it at nothing before you migrate off FreshBooks.
  • Promotional pricing varies and the jump from Solo to Pro at $70 a month regular AUD is steep.

Price: at the time of writing, Solo $99 per year regular and Pro $70 per month regular in AUD; promotional pricing varies. Best for: Australian sole traders who want invoicing, expenses and a tax estimate in Australian dollars.

4. Zoho Invoice — Best free option that actually sends

Zoho Invoice is free — Zoho states no cost and no contracts — and unlike a PDF generator it sends the invoice, saves the client and keeps the history. For a FreshBooks user whose real complaint is the monthly bill, this is the shortest route to A$0 without losing sending.

On the currency half of the test it does not so much pass as sidestep: there is no monthly figure at the time of writing, so there is nothing to convert and no exchange-rate exposure. No billable-client limit is listed, and the Australian site handles GST and ABN properly. The trade-off is the surroundings. Zoho Invoice is the front door to a very large product suite, the interface is dense next to a single-purpose invoicing app, and real support is a paid-suite affair. Free software with an upsell path is still free, but plan for the pitch.

Pros

  • Genuinely free with no contract, and it sends and stores invoices rather than only building a PDF.
  • Multi-language invoicing across 19-plus languages, which matters if you bill overseas clients.

Cons

  • The interface is dense, and it is the entry point to a large suite you will be encouraged to buy.
  • Support is really aimed at paid-suite customers, so you are on documentation for anything awkward.

Price: free at the time of writing. Best for: freelancers who want sending, tracking and a saved client list for nothing and can live with a busy interface.

5. Harvest — Best for billing by the hour

Harvest is the honest answer if time tracking is why you pay FreshBooks. It is a time tracker first and an invoicing tool second, and it turns tracked hours into an invoice without retyping anything. It also fails both halves of our test, which is worth knowing before you switch.

Pricing is in US dollars: the Teams plan starts at US$9 per seat per month on annual billing at the time of writing, so one seat is US$108 a year and the currency risk you left FreshBooks to escape comes straight back. The free plan is capped at 1 seat and 2 projects, and for a freelancer juggling five or six clients at once, a two-project ceiling is a harder limit than a billable-client count. GST handling is weak, so an Australian tax invoice needs checking against the ATO requirements rather than trusted.

Pros

  • Best-in-class time tracking that converts straight into an invoice, which is the FreshBooks feature people actually miss.
  • A genuinely free tier for one person on one or two jobs at a time.

Cons

  • Priced in US dollars, so it fails the whole reason most Australians look for a FreshBooks alternative.
  • The free plan stops at 1 seat and 2 projects, and GST handling is weak for Australian invoices.

Price: at the time of writing, free for 1 seat and 2 projects; Teams from US$9 per seat per month on annual billing. Best for: consultants who bill by the hour and will accept USD to keep proper time tracking.

6. Bonsai — Best for contracts and proposals alongside invoicing

Bonsai is a freelance all-in-one: contracts, proposals and invoicing in one subscription. If half your admin is getting a scope agreed and signed before work starts, it replaces more than FreshBooks does.

It fails the currency half outright. Bonsai runs USD $24 to $89 per month across four plans at the time of writing, which is US$288 to US$1,068 a year, and it is priced for US freelancers rather than Australian ones. No billable-client limit is listed, so it passes the second half. Australian GST handling is not something we could confirm, so treat the invoice template as a starting point and check it against the ATO list before you send. For an Australian sole trader whose contracts are already handled by an accountant or a template, this is a lot of software in the wrong currency.

Pros

  • Contracts, proposals and invoices in one place, which covers more of the job than an invoicing app.
  • No billable-client limit listed, so the client list can grow without a forced upgrade.

Cons

  • USD $24 to $89 per month at the time of writing, priced and shaped for US freelancers.
  • No Australian GST specialisation we could verify, so tax invoice compliance is on you.

Price: at the time of writing, USD $24 to $89 per month across four plans. Best for: freelancers who need proposals and contracts as much as invoices and work with overseas clients anyway.

7. Xero — Best if your bookkeeper already uses it

Xero is accounting software, not invoicing software, and that distinction decides whether it belongs on your shortlist. It is the right answer when a bookkeeper already works in Xero and wants invoices, bank feeds, GST and BAS in one system instead of three.

On the test it sidesteps the currency question — it sells through its Australian site with GST and BAS support, so you are not converting a US dollar bill — but we did not verify a figure we could quote, so we are not quoting one. No billable-client limit is listed. There is no free plan. For a solo freelancer who just wants to stop paying FreshBooks in US dollars, this is a bigger, slower and more expensive system than the problem calls for. With staff, a bookkeeper and a quarterly BAS, the maths flips, because you stop paying someone to re-key your invoices into a ledger.

Pros

  • Invoicing, bank reconciliation, GST and BAS in one system your accountant already knows.

Cons

  • No free plan, and no price figure we could verify at the time of writing.
  • Considerably more software than a solo operator leaving FreshBooks actually needs.

Price: paid plans available — check current pricing. Best for: businesses whose books are already run in Xero by a bookkeeper.

8. Square Invoices — Best if clients will only pay by card

Square Invoices passes both halves of the test and costs nothing to start: unlimited free invoices, estimates and automatic reminders, priced in Australian dollars, with no billable-client limit. The client pays by tapping a card link in the email.

Pros

  • Unlimited free invoices with automatic reminders, in Australian dollars, with no client cap.
  • Clients pay by card straight from the invoice, so money often lands the same day.

Cons

  • 2.2% on online card payments is A$1,760 a year on A$80,000 of card-paid invoices.
  • Estimate auto-convert and milestone schedules are Plus only, at AUD $30 per month.

Price: at the time of writing, free, or Plus at AUD $30 per month; 2.2% on online card payments. Best for: service businesses whose clients pay by card and who want the money in fast.

What to look for in a FreshBooks alternative in Australia

The trap when replacing FreshBooks is matching its feature list instead of matching what you actually use. Most people use three things: invoices out, reminders sent, and either hours or expenses attached. Everything else on the plan comparison is why you are paying US$276 a year.

  • A price in Australian dollars. A USD subscription is a small monthly bet on the exchange rate that you never agreed to place. Four of the eight tools here quote AUD; two quote USD; one is free.
  • No limit on billable clients. A cap turns your best month into an upgrade. Check whether the limit counts active clients, archived ones, or both, because the difference decides your tier.
  • Australian tax invoice fields by default. Where you are registered for GST the invoice must show the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. Generic GST handling means you configure that yourself; the full tax invoice requirements are worth reading before you migrate.
  • Whether you still need time tracking. If you bill hours logged in the app, only Harvest and FreshBooks itself really cover it here, and both are USD. If you bill fixed prices, you are paying for a feature you do not open.
  • The payment fee, not the subscription. PayID and bank transfer cost nothing and clear overnight. Card at around 2.2% costs A$1,760 on A$80,000 of billings, which dwarfs every subscription on this page.
  • What happens to your invoice history when you leave. Export before you cancel. On InvoiceSonic, historical invoices stay accessible after a downgrade and data is not deleted; do not assume that is standard.

Which one should you choose?

  • If you want FreshBooks-grade invoicing in Australian dollars with no client cap → InvoiceSonic on Pro, billed annually so the rate is locked.
  • If you want invoicing plus expenses and a tax estimate, Australian-built → Rounded on Solo, at $99 a year regular AUD.
  • If the monthly bill is the whole problem → Zoho Invoice, free at the time of writing, and accept the dense interface.
  • If you bill by the hour and time tracking is non-negotiable → Harvest, and budget for USD.
  • If you send two or three invoices a month → the free Australian tax invoice generator, and email the PDF yourself. Our pick of the best invoice generators for freelancers compares the free options in more detail.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. If time tracking and expense capture are the reason FreshBooks is on your card statement, InvoiceSonic does not replace them — it has no timesheets, no time tracking and no per-job cost tracking, and Harvest or FreshBooks itself is the better tool even in US dollars. If you want tax and expenses handled alongside invoicing in Australian dollars, Rounded is built here for sole traders and Solo is $99 a year regular AUD at the time of writing; InvoiceSonic Pro is US$14.99/month on monthly billing. If a bookkeeper runs your books and lodges your BAS, that is accounting software territory — Xero or MYOB, not an invoicing app, and no invoicing app will change that. And a free PDF generator, ours included, is the wrong choice the moment you need invoices sent, tracked and chased on their own, or the moment the same invoice has to go out every month.
