How we tested: the treatment-record test. A pest job produces paperwork that outlives the invoice. Three questions. Does the tool keep the job's own record — what was applied and where, your licence number, the warranty period you offered — with the job, or in a separate folder? Does the invoice carry enough detail that a property manager reading it eighteen months later can tell what was done? And does it schedule the next visit, so the annual termite inspection rebills itself?

We will not guess at your obligations. Pest management licensing is state-based, the licence goes by different names in different jurisdictions, and your state regulator sets the record-keeping rules — check what yours requires. None of these eight tools has a pest-specific licence field, so the number goes in your business details block or an invoice line.

The worked number that decides your payment method. Termite management is the big-ticket line in this trade, and the payment method costs more than the software. Say a termite job invoices at $3,600. Paid by card at Square's 2.2%, that is $79.20 gone on one job. Twelve a year is $43,200 of billings and $950.40 in card fees. The same twelve paid by bank transfer or PayID cost nothing: the money moves bank to bank and InvoiceSonic takes no transaction fee. At A$168 a year for Pro billed annually at the time of writing, that is more than five years of the subscription.

The worked number that decides your software. The annual inspection is the retention play. A book of 120 inspection clients at $330 a visit is $39,600 a year that arrives only if someone sends the reminder — six hours of manual rebilling at three minutes each, and every client you forget is $330 that walks to whoever did remember.

Who fails the test. Nothing passes all three questions. InvoiceSonic and Square handle the billing cycle but hold no job record. ServiceM8, Tradify and Fergus hold the record but did not state an automatic recurring send on the pages we checked. A free PDF generator fails two of three: it builds a compliant document and stops.

It passes two of the three questions and fails the middle one. Deposits earn their keep on big jobs: take a percentage or fixed amount up front on a termite job and raise the balance separately, so you are not funding the work. Quotes convert to invoices in one click on Pro. Clients pay by bank transfer or PayID with no gateway to set up and no InvoiceSonic transaction fee, and ABN, BSB and 10% GST labels are built in. What to look for in an invoicing app for pest control

Pest billing has two halves most software treats as one: a job that produces a record, and a customer who should come back in twelve months.

  • Recurring invoicing, not a saved template. A saved template still needs you to open the app, change the date and press send. On 120 annual inspections that is the whole $39,600.
  • Deposits and staged payments. A termite job with a real product cost should not be funded out of your own account. Look for a deposit up front, and check whether staged claims sit behind a higher tier.
  • Room for the detail on the invoice. The site, the service, your state's pest management licence number and the next inspection date should be readable on the invoice a property manager opens in eighteen months. Free-text line descriptions do that; a rigid template does not.
  • A proper Australian tax invoice. Where you are registered for GST it must carry the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of what was supplied, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more — which most termite jobs are. The full list of tax invoice requirements is also on the ATO site.
  • The payment method and its fee. Bank transfer and PayID cost nothing and clear overnight; card at 2.2% costs $79.20 on a $3,600 job, so price it in rather than absorb it.
  • Automatic reminders on the overdue ones. Commercial work runs on 30-day terms, so a reminder on the due date and another a week later does the chasing; our guide on how to chase overdue invoices in Australia covers the rest.

  • If you need scheduling and job records and cannot spend anything → ServiceM8's free plan, under 30 jobs a month.
  • If you have two or more technicians and need hours per job → Tradify, with the per-user cost priced in.
  • If product cost per job is what you cannot see → Fergus, once someone in an office runs it.

InvoiceSonic does no job cards, no scheduling, no timesheets and no materials tracking, so a business that needs the visit history attached to the address belongs on ServiceM8, Tradify or Fergus. With technicians on the books and a quarterly BAS, that is accounting software territory — Xero or MYOB.
