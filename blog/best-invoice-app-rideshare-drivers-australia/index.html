How we tested: the GST-from-day-one test. Three questions per tool. Does it issue a proper Australian tax invoice, with your ABN and a 10% GST line, on the first job rather than once you cross a turnover threshold? Does it keep a saved record you can total for a quarterly BAS? And does it get the one eleventh out of your account before you spend it?

The rule most articles about this get wrong. Taxi, ride-sourcing and rideshare drivers must register for GST regardless of turnover. The A$75,000 threshold that applies to nearly every other sole trader does not apply to you. Drive one Friday night for $180 and you are meant to hold an ABN, be registered and be charging 10% GST. The ATO sets that out in its ride-sourcing guidance. If you have been waiting to hit $75,000, read our note on the GST registration threshold and treat yourself as the exception.

The number that follows from that. Fares are GST-inclusive, so the GST inside them is one eleventh, not 10% of the total. On $48,000 of gross fares that is $48,000 ÷ 11 = $4,363.64, about $4,364 a year, $1,091 a quarter, or roughly $84 a week that was never yours. GST credits on fuel, servicing, tyres, the phone plan and platform fees come off that at BAS time, but the one eleventh is what you set aside first. Check a single fare with our GST calculator.

Who fails the test. Question one is a pass across the board: every tool here produces a tax invoice with an ABN and a GST line. Question two thins the field: a free PDF generator, ours included, saves nothing. Question three is where most invoicing apps fail honestly, InvoiceSonic among them — only Thriday and Hnry separate the GST from your money as it arrives, and Xero reports and lodges rather than sets aside.

One thing specific to rideshare. Uber, DiDi and Ola each send drivers a weekly statement, so you are not raising an invoice per passenger, and nothing in this list plugs into those platforms. Invoices are for the work beside the app: corporate accounts, direct airport transfers, hotel and function jobs. A payer who cannot see an ABN may be required to withhold 47%, so check whether you need an ABN to invoice.

What to look for in an invoice app for rideshare drivers

The platform does most of your billing. What you are buying is compliance, record-keeping for the work beside the app, and a way to survive a BAS you were signed up to on trip one.

  • GST on the invoice from trip one. There is no $75,000 runway for taxi and ride-sourcing work, so a tool that hides GST behind an "are you registered yet?" question works against you.
  • Records you can total for a quarter. A saved invoice history is the difference between a BAS that takes twenty minutes and one that takes a Sunday.
  • Expense and GST-credit capture. Fuel, servicing, tyres, car washes, the phone plan and the platform's service fee all carry credits that cut the $4,364.
  • A tax invoice the ATO would accept. It must carry the words "Tax invoice", your identity and ABN, the date, a description of the trip, the GST amount, and the buyer's identity or ABN for sales of $1,000 or more. The full list of tax invoice requirements is on this site and at the ATO.
  • Somewhere for the one eleventh to go. Only Thriday and Hnry do this as a product feature. Everything else relies on you moving about $84 a week yourself.
  • The payment method and its cut. Bank transfer and PayID cost nothing and clear overnight; a card fee of a couple of per cent on a $260 transfer is money you never see again.

Which one should you choose?

  • If nearly all your income is platform fares and you invoice a few private jobs a year → the free rideshare invoice template, and keep your own spreadsheet.
  • If you have corporate accounts or repeat private clients → InvoiceSonic on Pro, for the permanent history and quote-to-invoice conversion.
  • If you will spend the GST unless someone takes it first → Hnry, with the $528 a year written off as insurance.
  • If you are happy to change where your fares are paid → Thriday, free under A$50,000 of revenue at the time of writing, with the BAS automated.
  • If you want sending and a saved client list for nothing → Zoho Invoice, and accept the dense interface.

When not to use InvoiceSonic or a free generator. Neither is a tax account, and for a driver that is the gap that matters. InvoiceSonic does no bank feeds, no bookkeeping and no BAS lodgement, so if the GST is always gone by the end of the quarter, Hnry withholding it per payment or Thriday categorising it as it lands will do more for you than a better-looking invoice. If you want expenses and a tax estimate too, Solo App is Australian-built for sole traders. Run several cars or pay other drivers and you are in accounting software territory: Xero or MYOB with a bookkeeper. And a free PDF generator, ours included, is wrong the moment you need to prove what you invoiced three quarters ago.

Frequently asked questions

Do rideshare drivers have to register for GST in Australia?

Yes, from the first trip. Taxi, ride-sourcing and rideshare drivers must register for GST regardless of turnover, so the A$75,000 threshold that applies to other sole traders does not apply to you. You need an ABN, a GST registration and 10% GST on the fare before you take your first passenger.

What is the best invoice app for rideshare drivers in Australia?

InvoiceSonic. It issues an ATO-shaped tax invoice with your ABN and a 10% GST line from the very first job, and it keeps every invoice in a cloud history you can total at BAS time. If you want the GST actually withheld and paid for you rather than recorded, Hnry does that instead, for 1% + GST of every payment into your Hnry account at the time of writing.

Do I need to send invoices if I drive for Uber, DiDi or Ola?

Not for the platform fares. Uber, DiDi and Ola each send drivers a weekly statement, so you are not invoicing passengers trip by trip. You do need proper tax invoices for private bookings, corporate accounts, direct airport transfers and anything billed to a business, and you need records of the lot for your quarterly BAS.

How much GST will I owe on my rideshare income?

One eleventh of your GST-inclusive fares. On $48,000 of gross fares that is $4,363.64, so about $4,364 a year, roughly $1,091 a quarter, or $84 a week set aside. GST credits on fuel, servicing, car washes, the phone plan and platform fees come off that at BAS time, but the one eleventh is the figure to put away first.
