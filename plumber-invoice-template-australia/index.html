What to put on a plumber invoice

Common lines on a plumber's invoice include:

  • Labour — hours × rate
  • Fixtures and fittings supplied (taps, pipes, hot water unit)
  • Call-out fee
  • After-hours / emergency rate
  • Excavation or trenching
  • Travel and disposal

Plus the essentials every Australian invoice needs: your business name and ABN, the client's details, a unique invoice number and dates, the total (with 10% GST if you're registered), and your payment details. See how to write an invoice and the tax invoice requirements.

A tip for plumbers

Emergency and after-hours work usually carries a higher rate — put it on its own line so the client sees exactly what they're paying for. Keep a copy of every invoice for five years, as the ATO requires.

GST and your ABN

GST registration depends on your turnover and the work you do. For most businesses, the ATO registration threshold is $75,000 in current or projected annual GST turnover; ride-sourcing has a different rule. If you are GST-registered and the supply is taxable, show GST and use “Tax Invoice”. If you are not registered, use “Invoice” and do not charge GST. See our GST guide and ABN guide.

Frequently asked questions

What goes on a plumber's invoice?

Your name and ABN, the job address, itemised labour and materials, any call-out or after-hours fee, the total with GST if registered, and your payment details.

Should I charge a call-out fee on the invoice?

If you charge one, list it as a separate line so it's transparent. Agreeing it before the job avoids disputes.
