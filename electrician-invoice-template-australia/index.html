What to put on a electrician invoice

Common lines on an electrician's invoice include:

  • Labour — hours on site × rate
  • Materials (cabling, switches, fittings, switchboard components)
  • Certificate of Electrical Safety / compliance certificate
  • Call-out fee
  • After-hours or emergency loading
  • Travel

Plus the essentials every Australian invoice needs: your business name and ABN, the client's details, a unique invoice number and dates, the total (with 10% GST if you're registered), and your payment details. See how to write an invoice and the tax invoice requirements.

A tip for electricians

List any compliance or safety certificate as its own line — clients (and their insurers) often need to see it itemised. If you sub-contract to builders, quote your ABN on every invoice so they aren't forced to withhold 47% of your pay.

GST and your ABN

GST registration depends on your turnover and the work you do. For most businesses, the ATO registration threshold is $75,000 in current or projected annual GST turnover; ride-sourcing has a different rule. If you are GST-registered and the supply is taxable, show GST and use “Tax Invoice”. If you are not registered, use “Invoice” and do not charge GST. See our GST guide and ABN guide.

Frequently asked questions

What should an electrician's invoice include?

Your business name and ABN, the client and job address, itemised labour and materials, any compliance certificate, the total with 10% GST if you're registered, and payment details.

Do I need to show GST on an electrical invoice?

Yes if you're registered for GST (mandatory over $75,000 turnover). Show 10% GST and head the document 'Tax Invoice'.

Can I add my licence number to the invoice?

Yes — add it to the notes or your business details. It reassures clients the work was done by a licensed electrician.
