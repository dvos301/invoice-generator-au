What to put on a tutor invoice

Common lines on a tutor's invoice include:

  • Tutoring — sessions or hours × rate
  • Group session rate
  • Materials or workbooks
  • Travel to the student's home
  • Online session
  • Prepaid lesson block

Plus the essentials every Australian invoice needs: your business name and ABN, the client's details, a unique invoice number and dates, the total (with 10% GST if you're registered), and your payment details. See how to write an invoice and the tax invoice requirements.

A tip for tutors

Selling lesson blocks (e.g. 10 sessions prepaid) smooths your income — invoice the block up front and note the sessions covered. Most tutors stay under the $75,000 GST threshold, so a plain invoice with no GST is usually fine.

GST and your ABN

GST registration depends on your turnover and the work you do. For most businesses, the ATO registration threshold is $75,000 in current or projected annual GST turnover; ride-sourcing has a different rule. If you are GST-registered and the supply is taxable, show GST and use “Tax Invoice”. If you are not registered, use “Invoice” and do not charge GST. See our GST guide and ABN guide.

Frequently asked questions

Do tutors need to charge GST?

Only if you turn over $75,000 or more a year. Most private tutors are under the threshold and issue plain invoices with no GST.

What should a tutoring invoice include?

Your name and ABN, the parent/student, the sessions or hours and rate, the total, and your payment details.
