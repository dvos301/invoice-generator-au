What to put on a cleaner invoice

Common lines on a cleaner's invoice include:

  • Cleaning service — hours × rate, or per-clean flat fee
  • End-of-lease / bond clean
  • Carpet or window cleaning
  • Cleaning products and consumables
  • Travel between sites
  • Regular weekly/fortnightly clean

Plus the essentials every Australian invoice needs: your business name and ABN, the client's details, a unique invoice number and dates, the total (with 10% GST if you're registered), and your payment details. See how to write an invoice and the tax invoice requirements.

A tip for cleaners

For recurring clients, use the same invoice each period and just change the date and number — the generator auto-increments it. Set short payment terms (7 days) so weekly and fortnightly cleans don't pile up as unpaid.

GST and your ABN

GST registration depends on your turnover and the work you do. For most businesses, the ATO registration threshold is $75,000 in current or projected annual GST turnover; ride-sourcing has a different rule. If you are GST-registered and the supply is taxable, show GST and use “Tax Invoice”. If you are not registered, use “Invoice” and do not charge GST. See our GST guide and ABN guide.

Frequently asked questions

Do cleaners need an ABN to invoice?

If you're running a cleaning business, yes — quote your ABN or the client may have to withhold 47% of the payment. ABNs are free to get.

How do I invoice for a regular cleaning job?

Issue an invoice each clean (or each week/fortnight) with the date, a description, the amount and GST if registered. The tool remembers your details so it's quick to repeat.
