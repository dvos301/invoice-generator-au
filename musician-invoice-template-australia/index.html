What to put on a musician invoice

Common lines on a musician's invoice include:

  • Performance fee (per gig or per hour)
  • Rehearsal or session time
  • Travel and accommodation
  • Equipment or PA hire
  • Additional musicians / band members
  • Booking deposit already paid

Plus the essentials every Australian invoice needs: your business name and ABN, the client's details, a unique invoice number and dates, the total (with 10% GST if you're registered), and your payment details. See how to write an invoice and the tax invoice requirements.

A tip for musicians

Take a deposit to hold the date for weddings and events, and record it against the final invoice. Most working musicians stay under the $75,000 GST threshold, so a plain invoice with no GST is usually fine — but check as you grow.

GST and your ABN

GST registration depends on your turnover and the work you do. For most businesses, the ATO registration threshold is $75,000 in current or projected annual GST turnover; ride-sourcing has a different rule. If you are GST-registered and the supply is taxable, show GST and use “Tax Invoice”. If you are not registered, use “Invoice” and do not charge GST. See our GST guide and ABN guide.

Frequently asked questions

Do musicians need to charge GST?

Only if you turn over $75,000 or more a year. Many musicians are under the threshold and issue plain invoices with no GST.

What should a musician's invoice include?

Your name and ABN, the client or venue, the performance details and fee, any travel or extras, the total, and your payment details.
